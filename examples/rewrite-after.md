# Getting more restaurant reviews, and using them

Diners check Google and Zomato before they pick a place, and a page whose newest review is two years old reads like a restaurant that has closed. [NEEDS: the client's current review count and the date of the newest review, so this opening is about them]

The time to ask for a review is when a guest has just said something nice. If a table tells the server the mutton was the best they've had this year, that is the moment for "would you mind saying that on Google?" Asking at the bill is too late, because people are tired and already reaching for their phones to pay. Staff need only one sentence for this, and it helps to agree on that sentence together.

Make it one tap. A QR code on the table tent or at the bottom of the printed bill should open the review form itself, not the restaurant's profile, because every extra screen loses people. For delivery and takeaway, the thank-you message after the order is the natural place for the same link. [NEEDS: how the client's guests order and pay, so this matches their setup]

Answer the bad reviews first, and answer them specifically. "You're right about the cold dal on Saturday; we've stopped holding it on the counter" tells the next reader more than a page of five-star ratings, because it shows that someone reads the reviews and something changed. A reply that only says "Sorry for your experience" tells them nothing. Good reviews need a line at most.

Then read the reviews in batches, once a month, and count what repeats. Three people in one month mentioning a forty-minute wait for mains is a kitchen problem with a date on it. One complaint about the music is one person's taste. [NEEDS: the client's most common complaint and most common praise from recent reviews, if they have them]
