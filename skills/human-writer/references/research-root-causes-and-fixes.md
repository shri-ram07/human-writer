# Training makes AI typical; inputs make it original

AI-text detectors catch machine prose because training makes it measurably typical, plagiarism checkers rarely catch it because it is freshly composed, and the one fix that holds up with expert readers is to give the model information and a voice it does not have rather than to disguise what it produced. Detectors measure three things: how predictable each word is to a language model and how evenly that predictability is spread, along with the noun-heavy, evaluative register that instruction tuning leaves behind; whether runs of eight or more normalized words already exist in an index; and whether a keyed watermark is present, which Anthropic now applies to output from current Claude models worldwide, API included. The root causes sit in how models are trained and used. Decoding keeps every token in the head of the probability distribution, human raters prefer familiar text (an estimated typicality weight of 0.57–0.65), and the RLHF objective turns that mild preference into a sharpened distribution that behaves like a lowered temperature, so one model gives near-identical answers to **79%** of open-ended prompts and different vendors' models still overlap at **71–82%**. A model also has no first-hand knowledge, which is why professional authors score **66.2%** higher than LLMs on a measure of how much of a text cannot be rebuilt from the web. The fixes with measured effects act on inputs, the model and selection: the author's own facts and opinions, "propose several distinct directions, then choose" prompting (1.6–2.1x more semantic diversity), about five of the author's own samples, targeted repair of the faults professional editors fix, an outside quality scorer, and, strongest of all, fine-tuning on the author's own corpus, which moved expert readers from rejecting the AI's style (odds ratio 0.16) to preferring it (8.16) and cut Pangram's AI flags from 97% to 3% without ever aiming at Pangram. Temperature, word bans, paraphrasing "humanizers" and training against a detector either fail with readers or create new tells. For a founder in India the market and the law point the same way: humanizers are a bootstrapped, student-driven, high-churn business that cheating statutes, FTC substantiation rules, Google Ads, the model vendors' usage policies and EU marking rules are all closing, while agencies, brands and publishers already pay $65–$200 a month per team for half of what they need and freelancers lose clients over detector flags. India imposes no labelling duty on text today, but an Indian company that sells to EU users under its own brand is an EU AI Act "provider" whose outputs must stay machine-marked, and India's data-protection duties on writing samples and voice profiles take effect in May 2027.

## Detectors score typicality, checkers count copied runs, watermarks need a key

Every tool sold to catch AI writing or copying computes one of three quantities, and none of them is authorship: how typical a text looks to a model of machine output, how much of it overlaps something the tool can retrieve, or whether it carries a keyed mark from a cooperating generator. AI prose scores high on typicality and, when it comes from Claude in 2026, carries a mark; it scores low on overlap, because a language model composes new word sequences. Of the three channels, only typicality responds to how prose reads, and the most useful finding about it is that it measures the fingerprint of training.

### Typicality detectors read the imprint of post-training

Zero-shot detectors run a passage through a scoring model and read the probability or rank of each word that actually appears. The 2019 GLTR tool made the signal visible: human text uses words outside GPT-2's top 100 predictions **2.41 times** as often as GPT-2 text does ([Gehrmann, Strobelt & Rush 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.04043)). Fast-DetectGPT turns this into a z-score that compares each observed token's log-probability with tokens the scorer would have sampled itself, and reaches **0.9887 AUROC** with white-box access and 0.9061 on GPT-4 text ([Bao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.05130)). Binoculars divides one model's perplexity by its cross-perplexity with a sibling model, because an unusual prompt can make any completion look surprising; it caught over 90% of ChatGPT samples at a 0.01% false-positive rate, yet placed the US Constitution on the machine side, since memorized text is typical to every model ([Hans et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12070)). The 2026 methods name their target outright. LAPD scores the gap between an instruction-tuned model and its own base model, on the theory that alignment "reallocates probability mass toward a narrower set of preferred responses, causing entropy contraction," and it beats Fast-DetectGPT on average AUROC, 92.37 to 82.26 ([LAPD 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923v1)). A detector of this kind is a post-training detector.

Supervised classifiers learn the same probability structure plus the register that comes with it. Pangram trains on about 28 million pre-2021 human documents, each paired with an AI "mirror" matched in topic, length and style, and repeatedly retrains on its own false positives ([Emi & Spero 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14873)); its fourth version claims a **0.0041%** false-positive rate on more than a million human texts, a vendor figure nobody outside the company has reproduced ([Pangram 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27183)). Because the mirrors hold topic constant, what such a classifier can learn is how predictable the words are and how they are arranged. Turnitin's VP of AI put the target in one phrase: writing that is "too consistently average" ([Washington Post via AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/reports/2901)). Turnitin scores overlapping segments of a few hundred words and refuses texts under 300 words ([Turnitin FAQ](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/28477544839821-Turnitin-s-AI-writing-detection-capabilities-FAQs)), and an independent NBER audit found Pangram the only tool that met a 0.5% false-positive cap without losing accuracy ([Jabarian & Imas 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34223)). The most accurate instrument is still a small panel of people who use LLMs often. Five such experts, voting by majority, misclassified **1 of 300** non-fiction articles (99.3% of AI articles caught, no human article flagged), while non-experts flagged 56.7% of AI articles and 51.7% of human ones; the experts cited vocabulary in 53.1% of their explanations, sentence structure in 35.9% and originality in 23.7% ([Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654)).

How these instruments fail confirms what they measure. A repetition penalty cut detector accuracy on the six-million-text RAID benchmark by up to **32 points**, and base models, which have not been instruction-tuned, are harder to catch than their chat versions: Binoculars caught 61% of Mistral-7B base text against 91% of Mistral-chat text at a 5% false-positive rate ([Dugan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07940); [RAID leaderboard](https://github.com/liamdugan/raid/tree/main/leaderboard/submissions)). Predictable human writers are the false-positive population: in 2023 seven detectors flagged **61.22%** of 91 human-written TOEFL essays, although re-tests of newer tools range from 0% to 23.1% ([Liang et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10382961/)). Base rates finish the argument. A detector that catches 90% of AI text at a 1% false-positive rate is right only 47.6% of the time when 1% of submissions are AI, the arithmetic behind Vanderbilt's decision to switch off Turnitin's indicator after estimating about 750 false labels on 75,000 papers ([Vanderbilt 2023](https://www.vanderbilt.edu/brightspace/2023/08/16/guidance-on-ai-detection-and-why-were-disabling-turnitins-ai-detector/)); Curtin switched it off from 1 January 2026 ([Curtin 2025](https://www.curtin.edu.au/news/oasis-news/update-on-turnitin-ai-detection-tool/)). A proven bound caps any single-document detector at AUROC ≤ ½ + TV − TV²/2, where TV is the total-variation distance between a population's writing and a model's ([Sadasivan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156)), so a score is a lead and never proof.

### Plagiarism checkers catch copying and miss sameness

Plagiarism checkers never model style. They normalize the text, hash every run of k tokens, keep the minimum hash in each window and index the result, a scheme that guarantees any shared run of w + k − 1 tokens is found ([Schleimer, Wilkerson & Aiken 2003](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/publications/papers/sigmod03.pdf)). Turnitin shows only matches of **8 words or longer** by default ([Turnitin filters guide](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/23539146689549)), compares them with what it says are 54 billion web pages and 2.4 billion student papers ([Turnitin Similarity](https://www.turnitin.com/products/similarity/); [Turnitin instructor deck](https://www.uc.pt/site/assets/files/2392165/originality_presentation_instructors.pdf)), and states that it "does not detect plagiarism" at all, only similarity ([Turnitin blog](https://in.turnitin.com/blog/does-turnitin-detect-plagiarism)). Rewording defeats the method by construction: the best system in the 2014 PAN competition scored 0.900 on verbatim copying but **0.561** on human summaries ([PAN 2014](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1180/CLEF2014wn-Pan-PotthastEt2014.pdf)), and 14 of 15 deployed tools missed translation plagiarism ([Foltýnek et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41239-020-00192-4)). Freshly generated text passes easily. ChatGPT-written medical abstracts had a median iThenticate similarity of **27** against 100 for the indexed originals ([Gao et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-023-00819-6)), and 150 ChatGPT essays averaged 22% Turnitin similarity, below the 33% of 150 human essays ([Revista eCiencias](https://revistas.ucr.ac.cr/index.php/reciencias/article/view/11700)). The copying risk that remains is memorization of heavily duplicated text, which grows with model size, duplication and prompt length ([Carlini et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.07646)) and produced verbatim passages of 256 or more characters in 0.47–0.7% of unprompted GPT-2 generations ([Lee et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.07618)). A clean similarity report therefore says the text was not copied. It says nothing about whether the text adds anything, and that gap is where AI writing's originality problem lives.

### Watermarks sit outside the reach of style

A text watermark is a keyed statistical test. The KGW scheme hashes the previous token to split the vocabulary into green and red lists, nudges the green ones, and detects with a z-test that needs only the key ([Kirchenbauer et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10226)); Google's SynthID-Text runs keyed tournaments and showed no change in user ratings across about 20 million live Gemini responses ([Dathathri et al. 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08025-4)). Since August 2026 Anthropic marks output of current Claude models "wherever Claude is offered, worldwide," API included, with no opt-out mentioned ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content)). OpenAI marks ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU and keeps API watermarking "off by default" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/eu-text-provenance/)). Google names only "the Gemini app and web experience," and no public page says whether Gemini API output is marked ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/synthid/); [Google AI Developers Forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/does-gemini-api-text-output-carry-synthid-watermarking-gemini-2-5-flash-lite-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-eu-ai-act-art-50-2/177241)). The marks survive light edits and lose to full rewrites. Anthropic says light editing "probably won't remove the watermark completely; a complete rewrite where every word is replaced will" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)), and OpenAI reports about 80% detection on 200-token passages and 95% on 400-token passages at a 1% false-positive rate, with 10% synonym replacement cutting detection from about 92% to 66% ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/eu-text-provenance/)). The one independent test found open-source SynthID catching only **39%** of 200-token texts at a 1% false-positive rate ([Nemecek, Chaudhary & Ayday 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09604)). Detection is key-gated (Anthropic's detection API is in private preview for regulators, media, researchers and educational organizations), so commercial detectors cannot read the mark, and no change of style moves it.

| Instrument | What it measures | How AI prose shows up | What moves it | What it cannot see |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zero-shot statistics (Fast-DetectGPT, Binoculars, LAPD) | Token probability and rank under a scoring model, relative to what that model expected | Words consistently in the head of the distribution; flat surprisal; the alignment imprint | Decoding settings, repetition penalties, base models, paraphrase | Predictable human writing (second-language, legal, memorized text); depends on the scorer |
| Supervised classifiers (Pangram, GPTZero, Turnitin) | Learned probability structure and register against topic-matched human text | Instruction-tuned register, uniform structure, humanizer traces | Retraining on new generators and humanizers | Very short texts, mixed authorship, unfamiliar genres |
| Expert readers | Vocabulary, sentence structure, originality, specifics | Evaluative vocabulary, template structure, generic content | Little: humanized text is still caught | Scale and cost |
| Plagiarism checkers (Turnitin Similarity, iThenticate, Copyleaks) | Runs of about 8+ normalized words present in an index | Rarely flagged, because the text is new | Paraphrase, translation, unindexed sources | Semantic sameness and idea reuse |
| Watermarks (Claude, SynthID-Text, textGrain) | Correlation of token choices with a secret key | Present in Claude output worldwide and EU ChatGPT and Codex text | Full regeneration, heavy paraphrase, translation | Short or low-entropy text; anyone without the key |

The three channels give a writing product three separate jobs. Overlap is handled by composing instead of reproducing and by checking long spans against public indexes. The watermark is a provenance fact to leave intact. Typicality is the only channel that depends on how the prose reads, and its causes sit in training.

## Preference tuning turns a mild taste for the familiar into 1,000x tells

Ten causes explain almost every tell and almost all of the sameness, and they fall into four groups: how decoding samples, what preference optimization rewards, how data flows between models and the web, and the plain fact that a model knows only what has already been written. The causes of recognizable surface style shift with every release. The causes of sameness run deeper, and they are getting worse.

### Decoding keeps every token in the probability head

"Natural language rarely remains in a high probability zone for multiple consecutive time steps," Holtzman and colleagues observed; on GPT-2 Large, human text had a perplexity of 12.38 under the model while greedy decoding produced 1.50, and the greedy outputs also resembled each other more (Self-BLEU 0.50 against 0.31) ([Holtzman et al. 2020](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.09751)). Every practical decoder cuts off the low-probability tail, because sampling from it produces incoherence, and in doing so it deletes the region where some human word choices live; top-k sampling at k = 40 put up to 80% of its probability mass on the 500 most common tokens ([Ippolito et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.00650)). The result is low average surprise and low variance of surprise: on information-density features, "the UID scores of human-written text have a higher mean and larger standard deviation than most machine-written text" ([Venkatraman, Uchendu & Lee 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06202)). Long-range novelty suffers too. Above four-word sequences model text is less novel than human text, and larger models and more constrained decoding lower novelty further ([Merrill, Smith & Elazar 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13069)). Grammatical templates come from pretraining: about 95% of model summaries contain repeated six-tag part-of-speech templates against 38% of human references, 76% of those templates appear in pretraining data, and they are "not overwritten during fine-tuning or alignment" ([Shaib et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00211v2)).

The useful inference concerns where human surprise comes from. Most surprisal spikes in human prose are information spikes: a proper name, an exact number, a local idiom, a quotation, a detail only the writer knows. Raising the temperature adds spikes in the wrong places, and in a controlled narrative study temperature was only weakly linked to novelty while moderately linked to incoherence ([Peeperkorn et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00492)). A truncated decoder cannot stumble on a human's idiosyncratic choice by accident. That kind of content has to enter through the context, where it becomes probable given the prompt and stays coherent, while still looking surprising to an outside observer who lacks the context.

### Raters reward the familiar, and RLHF raises the preference to a power

The largest single cause of sameness is how preference data is collected and optimized. Zhang and colleagues show that annotators "systematically favor familiar text," a bias they ground in the mere-exposure effect and processing fluency, and they estimate its weight on 6,874 correctness-matched HelpSteer pairs at **α = 0.57 ± 0.07** or 0.65 ± 0.07, depending on the model used to score typicality; on human-only preference data, raters picked the answer the base model found more probable 51.6–60.8% of the time ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171)). Under the KL-regularized RLHF objective the optimal policy becomes the base distribution raised to the power γ = 1 + α/β, which exceeds one whenever α is positive. Among equally good answers this "behaves like temperature scaling" toward the most typical ones, and it can produce collapse "even with a perfect reward model and optimization process" ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171)). The measurements agree with the theory. On Tulu-70B, the diversity of directly prompted output fell from 45.4% in the base model to 20.8% after supervised fine-tuning and 10.8% after DPO ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v3)); RLHF "significantly reduces output diversity compared to SFT" ([Kirk et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06452v3)); aligned models show less conceptual diversity than their instruction-tuned versions ([Murthy, Ullman & Hu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04427)); and alignment cuts a web-novelty "creativity index" by 30.1% on average ([Lu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04265)). OpenAI's own GPT-4 report says "the post-training hurts calibration significantly" ([OpenAI 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.08774)).

Two findings keep this from being an indictment of fine-tuning as such. Part of the measured loss is aggregation, since aligned answers fold several base-model answers into one longer answer ([Lake, Choi & Durrett 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.17692)). And more fine-tuning data from a target distribution "moves model diversity toward the human (or synthetic target) level in all experiments" ([Skobelev, Fithian & Han 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16454)). The cause is the objective and the data. Tuning on one person's writing is a different objective with different data, which is why it reappears below as the strongest fix.

### Six reward proxies build the assistant register

A second layer of rewarded proxies produces the recognizable assistant register, and each element maps onto a measured bias. Length: "a purely length-based reward reproduces most downstream RLHF improvements over supervised fine-tuned models" ([Singhal et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716)). Formatting: preference models favour "lists, links, bold text, and emojis," and less than 1% biased data is enough to bias a reward model ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11704)); in Chatbot Arena, controlling for length and markdown moved grok-2-mini from sixth place to eighteenth ([LMSYS 2024](https://lmsys.org/blog/2024-08-28-style-control/)). Agreement: five assistants "consistently exhibit sycophancy," and people prefer answers that match their own views ([Sharma et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)); OpenAI's April 2025 GPT-4o update added a thumbs-up reward that "weakened the influence of our primary reward signal, which had been holding sycophancy in check" and had to be rolled back ([OpenAI 2025](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/)). Confidence: models are "reluctant to express uncertainties," their confident answers were wrong 47% of the time, and human raters are "biased against texts with uncertainty" ([Zhou et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06730)). Positivity: "RLHF improves sentiment towards all groups" ([Bai et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.05862)). Prestige vocabulary: ChatGPT-3.5 used "delves" 183 times per million words against 3.2 on arXiv, which rules out pretraining frequency as the source; Llama 3.2-3B's instruct version used "nuanced" 8,342% more than its base model; and 400 raters preferred abstracts dense in LLM-associated words by 52.4% to 47.6% ([Juzek & Ward 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.11385v1); [Juzek & Ward 2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01930v1)).

Grammar follows the same path. Base Llama 3 sits near human rates for present participial clauses (94–102%), while GPT-4o uses them at **527%** of the human rate and nominalizations at about 209–214% ([Reinhart et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v1)). Alignment acts mostly on style and on the first tokens of a response: comparing Llama-2-7b with its chat version, 77.7% of token positions keep the base model's top choice, and the shifted ones include "However," "Great (question)," "Of (course)" and "Here (are some)" ([Lin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.01552)). Safety and character training add reflexive balance and caveats, which providers now list as failures; Anthropic's January 2026 constitution names "an unhelpful, wishy-washy response out of caution when it isn't needed" and "excessive warnings, disclaimers, or caveats" ([Anthropic constitution](https://www.anthropic.com/constitution)). Chat templates narrow output further, with diversity collapse persisting "even under high-temperature sampling" ([Yun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18949)). Repetition and frequency penalties, exposed by many APIs, discount any token already used, the likeliest source of synonym cycling: one vendor benchmark found that adjacent sentences in machine text share content words at a median rate of 0.021 against 0.063 in human text ([Opace 2026](https://opace.agency/tools/ai/content-verification-integrity/research/ai-writing-myths-measured/)).

The exponent is the most plausible route from mild preferences to loud tells, although no study has traced it end to end. A 52–60% preference per comparison, optimized over millions of comparisons and raised to a power above one, is consistent with word rates 57 times the human baseline ("delves" in ChatGPT-3.5 against arXiv) and phrase ratios above 1,000: in gemma-3-12b's creative writing the name "Elara" appeared 85,513 times more often than in human text, and "flickered" sits on 98.5% of 67 models' lists of over-used words ([Paech et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15061)). Reward models add a second lexical pull, overvaluing "more frequent tokens" ([Christian et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07326)), so a model converges on a small set of words that are impressive but safe. None of this is a design goal, since providers' own specifications reject most of it. These behaviours are side effects of the objective, which is why they move from version to version as individual symptoms get patched.

### Shared data and missing first-hand knowledge make every model write the same post

Sameness is the bigger problem for anyone selling "unique" content, and it has four stacked causes. First, one prompt hits one mode. Across 26,070 real open-ended queries, sampling a model 50 times per query at temperature 1.0 gave an average pairwise similarity above 0.8 in **79%** of cases, and even min-p sampling at temperature 2.0 left 61.2% of response pairs above 0.8 ([Jiang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)). Second, different models share modes. Responses from different models averaged **71–82%** similarity, two Qwen models produced the identical motto "Empower Your Journey: Unlock Success, Build Wealth, Transform Yourself," and 25 models asked for a metaphor about time collapsed into two clusters, "time is a river" and "time is a weaver" ([Jiang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.22954)). Eleven words, among them Elias, Mara, Elara, lighthouses and clockmakers, appear in **88.3%** of 20,000 stories from four current models; they are rare in published literature and pretraining data but present in preference data "likely used by all current models" ([Hamilton & Mimno 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26492)). Synthetic data spreads the style. Stanford's Alpaca was trained on 52,000 demonstrations generated by text-davinci-003 for under $500 ([Stanford CRFM 2023](https://crfm.stanford.edu/2023/03/13/alpaca.html)); fine-tuning on ChatGPT-generated UltraChat cut the separability of Llama and Gemma outputs from 96.5% to 59.8%, and a classifier labelled 82.8% of Grok's outputs as ChatGPT ([Sun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150)).

Third, a model has no first-hand information. It recombines written consensus, so professional authors score 66.2% higher than LLMs on a creativity index that measures how much of a text cannot be rebuilt from web snippets ([Lu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04265)), and LLM-modified peer reviews carried "less specific feedback and fewer citations" ([Liang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183v1)). Google's guidance targets exactly this property, asking whether content provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis" and analysis "beyond the obvious," and calling mass AI generation "without manual oversight or curation" "little to no effort" ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)); a Google patent scores documents by the "additional information" they contain beyond pages the user has already seen, though Google has never confirmed using it in ranking ([US 11,354,342 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11354342B2/en)). Fourth, the ecosystem feeds the consensus back in. A vendor estimate puts primarily AI-written articles at about half of new English web articles since late 2024, 49.9% in the first quarter of 2026 ([Graphite 2026](https://graphite.io/five-percent/ai-now-writes-as-many-online-articles-as-humans-do)); an unpublished study of 388 search keywords, reported secondhand, found page similarity rising between 2019 and 2024 because "everybody tries to mimic the top 10 performers" ([UNSW BusinessThink 2026](https://businessthink.unsw.edu.au/articles/ai-content-tools-seo-geo-search-visibility)); generative search engines prefer sources that are more predictable to LLMs ([Ma et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14436)); and recursive training on generated data makes the "tails of the original content distribution disappear" unless real data keeps accumulating ([Shumailov et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11269175/); [Gerstgrasser et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01413)).

The homogenizing effect reaches the people who write with these models. Writers given GPT-4 story ideas produced stories rated 8.1% more novel individually but more similar to each other ([Doshi & Hauser 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532)), and in co-written essays the homogenization came from InstructGPT's contributions, neither from the humans' own text nor from the base GPT-3 model ([Padmakumar & He 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3)). The conclusion for a product is direct. "Plagiarism-free" and "original" are different properties: model text is novel at the short n-gram lengths that plagiarism checkers inspect while being semantically redundant with the web and with every other model's output on the same topic. If a thousand customers ask for a blog post on one topic and supply nothing of their own, they will receive variations on a few angles, examples and metaphors, and those variations will resemble every competitor's output as well.

### Named tells fade within a generation while the mechanisms persist

Individual tells last about one model generation once they become public, because providers train them out. The odds ratio for "delve" in LLM-rewritten arXiv abstracts fell from over 300:1 for GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 to about 40:1 for GPT-4o ([Yakura et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.01754v3)); em dashes fell from 10.62 per 1,000 words in GPT-4.1 to 1.43 in GPT-5.4, against a human mean of 3.23, while Claude Opus 4.6 used 9.09 unconstrained and 0.19 under a no-markdown instruction ([Freeburg 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27006)). The fixes create replacements. Wikipedia's editors now note that for newer models "commas and periods are more likely now than even human text, and now that has become its own sign," with sentences held around 8–20 words ([Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)), and ChatGPT-favoured words such as "significant" kept rising after "delve" fell ([Geng & Trotta 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09606)). The mechanisms underneath persist. The "not X but Y" construction rose on the web from 0.87 to 2.36 per 10,000 words between 2023 and 2026 ([Pew Research Center 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/how-much-of-the-internet-is-written-with-ai.md)), and fixed-list "slop" scores roughly halved within model families over 2025–2026 (Claude Sonnet 4 at 2.7, Claude Opus 5 at 0.9), a decline that partly measures avoidance of the list itself ([EQ-Bench](https://eqbench.com/creative_writing.html)).

Model identity outlasts all of it. A classifier told ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Gemini and DeepSeek apart with **97.1%** accuracy, still scored 91.4% after GPT-4o-mini paraphrased the text and 91.8% after translation, and scored 88.9% with the words shuffled into random order, so the fingerprint lives mainly in which words a model picks ([Sun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150)). Two practical rules follow. Rewriting a draft with another LLM cannot remove a machine signature, because the original stays identifiable and the rewriter adds its own. And banning words leaves untouched the register that generates them.

The causal map below condenses the evidence; R2, R5, R7 and R8 drive sameness, while R3, R9 and R10 drive recognizable surface style.

| # | Root cause | Mechanism | Tells it produces | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Likelihood pretraining plus truncated decoding | Samples only the head of the distribution | Low and flat surprisal; part-of-speech templates; few novel long n-grams | Inherent to sampling |
| R2 | Typicality-biased preference optimization | Raises the base distribution to a power γ > 1 | The same answer, metaphor, angle and outline for the same prompt | High while preference data favours the familiar |
| R3 | Reward proxies: length, format, agreement, confidence, positivity, prestige words | Optimizes what raters and reward models favour | Padding and summaries; headers and bold bullets; flattery; uniform confidence; upbeat endings; participial, nominal register | Patched version by version |
| R4 | Safety and character specifications | Defaults to balance, disclaimers and reticence | Reflexive both-sides framing; caveats; moralizing | Being reduced by providers |
| R5 | Synthetic data, distillation, shared preference data | Copies one model's habits into others | Cross-vendor sameness; stock names; misattributed fingerprints | Rising |

| R7 | No first-hand access | Recombines written consensus | Generic claims, few citations, invented specifics when pushed | Fundamental |
| R8 | Ecosystem loops: AI web content, SEO imitation, retrieval bias, recursive training | Feeds the consensus back as input | Rising page similarity; vanishing tails | Worsening |

## The fixes that measurably work add information, voice and outside judgment

The levers fall into two families, and the evidence treats them very differently. Toward-human levers optimize against human writing, expert preference or expert edits, and use detectors only as measuring instruments. Against-detector levers optimize a detector's score. The first family has measured gains with expert readers and lowers detector scores as a side effect; the second lowers the targeted score and gains nothing with readers.

### Sampling knobs are weak, and frontier APIs have removed them

Temperature is the folk remedy, and it barely works. Five temperatures of one model could be told apart only 37.9% of the time (chance is 20%), against 97.1% for five different models, so the fingerprint belongs to the model rather than the setting ([Sun et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12150)). Min-p sampling's ICLR 2025 headline did not survive re-analysis: of twelve paired human-evaluation tests, one stayed significant after Bonferroni correction ([Schaeffer, Kazdan & Denisov-Blanch 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13681v2)). On frontier APIs the question is moot. Anthropic's migration guide for Claude Opus 5.5 says "Omit `temperature`, `top_p`, and `top_k`. Use prompting to guide the model's behavior" ([Claude Opus 5.5 migration guide](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/opus-5-5/migration-guide)), and tells callers who relied on temperature for design variety to have the model "propose 4 distinct ... directions" and then implement only one ([Anthropic migration guides](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/migration-guide)); OpenAI's guide tells developers to remove temperature and top_p whenever reasoning effort is not `none` ([OpenAI latest-model guide](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/latest-model)); and Vertex AI ignores custom sampling values and does not support custom penalties for Gemini 3.6 Flash and later ([Vertex AI](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/multimodal/content-generation-parameters)). Repetition penalties and high temperature do lower detector accuracy, but no study shows them improving expert-judged quality, so they belong to the against-detector family, and penalties cause the synonym cycling that expert readers notice.

### First-hand inputs are the only lever that adds information

Every cause of sameness in the map (R2, R5, R7, R8) is answered by information the model does not have, and the evidence for this lever is the most consistent in the review. When people co-write with an instruction-tuned model the homogenization comes from the model's contributions, while key points the human wrote stay diverse ([Padmakumar & He 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.05196v3)). "Lack of specificity and detail" is one of the seven fault categories professional writers fix in LLM prose ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509v5)). For an Indian founder one study matters more than most. With GPT-4o's inline suggestions, Indian participants' writing moved toward American style (d = −0.22) more than American writing moved toward Indian (d = −0.12), a classifier's accuracy at telling Indian from American authors fell from 90.6% to 83.5% (from 82.9% to 60% on emails), and AI-assisted essays about Diwali and biryani came out "more generic," with exoticising words such as "aromatic" ([Agarwal, Naaman & Vashistha 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11360v3)). Human-supplied substance also changes what content-based judges see: GPT-4.1 reviews written from bullet-point summaries of real human reviews were caught 5.8% of the time at a 0.1% false-positive rate by an embedding detector that caught 59.6–63.5% of fully AI-written reviews, although that detector compares content rather than wording ([Yu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19614)). Grounding also protects quality at the most basic level, since 55% of GPT-3.5's citations and 18% of GPT-4's were fabricated in one test ([Walters & Wilder 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5)). In practice the lever is an information-gain inventory built before drafting (the author's experiences, numbers, customer quotes, dates, places, failures and opinions), three to five targeted questions or visible placeholders when the inventory is empty, and no invented anecdote, statistic or quotation, ever.

### Diverge first, then let an outside scorer choose

To break the mode, ask for several genuinely different candidates before choosing one. Verbalized sampling, peer-reviewed at ICML 2026, asks the model for k responses with a stated probability for each, and raised semantic diversity in creative writing **1.6–2.1x** over direct prompting; in a 30-annotator study, ratings on a four-point scale rose from 1.90 to 2.39 for poems, from 2.74 to 3.06 for stories and from 1.83 to 3.01 for jokes, and on Tulu-70B the method kept 66.8% of the base model's diversity against 23.8% for direct prompting ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01171v3); [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/zhang26hy.html)). Quality drops as k grows, so four or five candidates is the working range, and the evidence comes from creative writing, so its transfer to marketing copy is untested. The choosing must not be left to the drafting model. General LLMs are close to chance at judging writing quality on a 4,729-pair benchmark (GPT-4o 40.9% zero-shot, o3 64.3%), while a ModernBERT reward model trained on professional edits reaches 74.3%; generating 20 candidate edits and keeping the one it scores highest produced edits that nine professional writers preferred 66% of the time, 72.2% when the score gap exceeded one point ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu 2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07532v3)). LLM selectors also prefer LLM writing, choosing GPT-4-written product descriptions 89% of the time where humans chose them 36% ([Laurito et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12337326/)); they recognize and favour their own output ([Panickssery, Bowman & Feng 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)); and they grow more biased toward their own style when they self-refine without outside feedback ([Xu et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.826)). The reward model's authors are candid about its ceiling, calling the method "no where near expert quality writing" ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu 2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07532v3)).

### The author's own text beats any persona, and training on it beats both

Voice comes from the author's own text. Across more than 400 everyday authors and six models, five samples of an author's writing produced imitations that authorship-verification models accepted as the author 94.7–97.5% of the time for news and 95.7–96.6% for email, but only 50.0–65.9% for Reddit posts and 16.7–21.3% for blogs; moving from two samples to ten changed results "very little," and samples chosen for topical similarity made attribution worse ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)). Asking a model to continue the author's own text reached 99.9% style agreement in another study, yet the matched essays had a perplexity of 15.2 against 29.5 for the humans: "high-fidelity imitation does not imply human-like unpredictability" ([Jemama & Kumar 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24930v1)). No study supports "write as <famous person>" personas for long-form style.

The largest effect in the whole review comes from training on the author. Chakrabarty, Ginsburg and Dhillon had MFA-trained readers compare imitations of 50 award-winning authors with imitations written by MFA-trained writers. Prompted GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Gemini 1.5 Pro lost badly with the experts (style odds ratio **0.16**, quality 0.13); GPT-4o fine-tuned on each of 30 authors' complete works won (style **8.16**, quality 1.87), beat parity on style for 27 of the 30 authors at a median cost of $81 per author, and cut Pangram's AI flag rate from 97% to 3% and GPTZero's from 91% to 0% without being trained against either ([Chakrabarty, Ginsburg & Dhillon 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13939v3)). The caveats are large: one preprint, literary fiction, novelists with whole books of training text, and a vendor platform that is closing, since OpenAI's fine-tuning service no longer accepts new users ([OpenAI model optimization guide](https://platform.openai.com/docs/guides/model-optimization)). A startup would have to reproduce the result with LoRA on open weights or with Gemini tuning on Vertex AI ([Vertex AI tuning](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/models/tune-models)), for customers who own far less text than a novelist, and nobody has tested that. Base models point the same way from another angle. Llama-3.1-70B base won every originality contest against two aligned versions of itself in a small poetry study but never won on pleasantness ([West & Potts 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.00047v2)), and base-model text is harder for every detector on RAID, but commercial APIs do not offer base models.

### Targeted repair beats ban lists and over-correction

The most direct evidence on what makes LLM prose read better comes from professional editors. Eighteen MFA-trained writers edited 1,057 paragraphs from GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Llama 3.1 70B and made 8,035 edits; awkward word choice and phrasing accounted for about 28%, poor sentence structure 20%, unnecessary exposition 18% and clichés 17%, and no model family wrote measurably better than another ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509v5)). An automated pass that finds such spans and rewrites only them closed about half the gap to professional edits (mean expert rank 1.99, against 2.51 for raw output and 1.50 for writer-edited text), and spans found by an LLM worked as well as the writers' own, so "detection of problematic spans is not the bottleneck"; the quality of the rewrite is ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.14509v5)). Ban lists work only when they are data-driven and enforced without breaking the text. Antislop profiles each model's phrases by their frequency relative to human text; its backtracking sampler suppressed more than 8,000 patterns, while plain token banning "becomes unusable at just 2,000," and its token-level fine-tune (FTPO) removed 92% of 2,000 targeted patterns with writing quality at 68.9 against a 67.8 baseline, where DPO on the same patterns dropped quality to 63.0 ([Paech et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.15061v2)). Those quality scores come from LLM judges only, and the sampler needs token log-probabilities that frontier APIs no longer return, so on Claude or GPT the practical version is a rewrite of flagged sentences. Constructions such as "not X but Y" respond to instruction: one line in the prompt cut the figure by half to three-quarters and a LoRA adapter removed it, with the author arguing for calibration to each genre's human rate rather than elimination ([Boggia 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21498)).

Over-correction is the trap on the other side. Forced rhythm does not help: sentence-length variation is near chance as a signal (AUROC 0.521), and few-shot exemplars of human rhythm produced the flattest output in seven of nine model families ([Opace 2026](https://opace.agency/tools/ai/content-verification-integrity/research/ai-writing-myths-measured/); [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/164820)). Punctuation bans produced the 2026 comma-and-period style that editors now flag. Lay readers trust the wrong cues: contractions read as human, yet they were more common in AI-written profiles, and first-person pronouns were non-diagnostic ([Jakesch, Hancock & Naaman 2023](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07271)). The durable rules target mechanisms instead: repeat a key term rather than cycling synonyms, prefer plain verbs to "serves as" and trailing participial clauses, give a list the number of items its content has, take punctuation rates from the author's own corpus, and keep every word list dated and checked against the current model.

### Optimizing against a detector fools that detector and no one else

Training a generator against a detector reliably fools that detector. Reinforcement learning with a detector's "human" score as reward cut an OpenAI RoBERTa detector's AUROC from 0.84 to 0.63 in under a day of training ([Nicks et al. 2024](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1f9f07df0992ce21698d800eaa891bd8-Abstract-Conference.html)), and DPO with human text as the preferred answer cut detectors by 5–35 points, Binoculars falling from 0.99 to 0.33 macro-F1 on one news set ([Pedrotti et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24523v1)). It does not fool the other instruments. Tuning Mistral-7B against Fast-DetectGPT dropped that detector's AUROC from 0.72 to 0.18 while a stylometric detector moved from 0.96 to 0.95, and the tuned model was "detectable with just 5 samples" ([Rivera Soto, Chen & Andrews 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14608v2)). In the DPO study human judges' majority accuracy rose from 0.52 to 0.70 on the tuned Llama output and fell from 0.79 to 0.65 on Gemma, and nobody measured whether the text still read well ([Pedrotti et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24523v1)). Commercial humanizers sit at the same dead end. On humanized o1-pro articles an expert majority still caught 100% and Pangram 90%, while GPTZero caught 46.7% ([Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654)); Pangram's own audit found that "the more readable/fluent the humanizer text is, the more likely it is to be detected," and that the outputs it missed were often too garbled to read as English ([Pangram 2025](https://www.pangram.com/blog/humanizers-aug-25)); and Turnitin trained its August 2025 model on "the signals and patterns of leading humanizers" ([Plagiarism Today 2025](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/08/27/turnitin-launches-anti-ai-humanizer-feature/)). The contrast with author fine-tuning is the cleanest result in this review: training toward a person's writing cut detector flags from 97% to 3% and won expert readers, while training toward a detector lowered only that detector's score.

| Rank | Lever | Best measured effect | Evidence | Works on commercial APIs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First-hand content anchors: the author's facts, numbers, anecdotes, opinions | Content-based detection 59.6–63.5% → 5.8%; human key points stay diverse | Mechanism replicated; detector effect from one study | Yes; needs author input |
| 2 | Detect-then-rewrite against the editors' seven fault categories | Closes about half the expert-rank gap to writer edits | Peer-reviewed (CHI 2025) | Yes |
| 3 | Best-of-N candidates reranked by an edit-trained quality model | Writers prefer the chosen edit 66%, 72% at large score gaps | Peer-reviewed (COLM 2025) | Drafting yes; scorer runs locally |
| 4 | Verbalized sampling: k distinct options with probabilities, pick a non-modal one | 1.6–2.1x semantic diversity; higher human ratings | Peer-reviewed (ICML 2026); creative writing only | Yes; Anthropic recommends the pattern |
| 5 | About five style-matched samples of the author, continuation format where possible | Verified as the author 95–97% (email, news), 17–21% (blogs) | Peer-reviewed (EMNLP Findings 2025) | Yes |
| 6 | Per-author fine-tune on the customer's corpus | Expert style odds ratio 0.16 → 8.16; Pangram flags 97% → 3% | One preprint; novelists; GPT-4o | Hard: open weights or Vertex AI |
| 7 | Model-specific over-use profile enforced by rewrite (APIs) or Antislop/FTPO (open weights) | About 90% fewer over-used patterns at equal judged quality | One preprint; LLM judges only | Post-hoc rewrite only |
| 8 | Research and outline before drafting long pieces | Articles judged organized 25% more often, 10% broader coverage ([Shao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207)) | Peer-reviewed | Yes |
| 9 | Expert human edit for flagship pieces | Writer-edited text ranked first 65% of the time; Binoculars AUROC 83.9 → 61.2 ([Beemo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04032)) | Peer-reviewed | As a service tier |
| Avoid | Temperature, min-p, penalties, word bans, unguided self-refine, humanizers, training against detectors | No quality gain; new tells or reader detection | Converging studies | Mostly unavailable anyway |

The ordering is a synthesis. Rows 2–5 carry the most direct peer-reviewed evidence, row 1 ranks first because it is cheap, supported by several mechanism studies, and the only lever that adds information, and row 6 has the largest single effect on the thinnest evidence and the hardest procurement.

### Measure with experts and novelty, and treat detectors as thermometers

No single instrument certifies "human-quality and original," so a product needs three. Quality and human-likeness are best judged by a small blind panel of professional writers who use LLMs often, the profile whose five-member majority caught 99.3% of AI articles with no false positives ([Russell, Karpinska & Iyyer 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654)), backed day to day by an edit-trained quality scorer rather than an LLM judge ([Chakrabarty, Laban & Wu 2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07532v3)). Originality needs its own instruments: novelty against web-scale corpora from n-gram counts, the creativity-index method, which the public infini-gram API supports ([Lu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04265v1); [infini-gram API](https://infini-gram.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api.html)); near-duplicate checks across customers with MinHash LSH ([datasketch](https://ekzhu.com/datasketch/lsh.html)); and within-brief diversity from compression ratio, long-n-gram self-repetition and Self-BLEU ([Shaib et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-demo.5)). Voice match can be scored with a content-independent style embedding such as StyleDistance ([StyleDistance model card](https://huggingface.co/StyleDistance/styledistance)), remembering that human texts match their own author's style model only 69–82% of the time ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)). Detectors belong in the kit only as thermometers: an ensemble thresholded at fixed 1% and 5% false-positive rates on genre-matched human text, reported detector by detector and never averaged into one "AI %." The open RAID leaderboard is saturated, with the best checkpoints catching 99.8–100% of AI text at a 5% false-positive rate, but its generators date from 2023, so those numbers say little about Claude 5.x or GPT-6 output, and some top checkpoints carry non-commercial licences ([RAID leaderboard](https://github.com/liamdugan/raid/tree/main/leaderboard/submissions)). The supervised detectors, the quality scorer and the style model all fit on a 6 GB laptop GPU. The decision rule that keeps a product on the toward-human side is simple: ship a pipeline change only when expert preference, the quality score, voice match and diversity do not fall, and reject any change whose only measured gain is a lower detector score.

## Money and law both point an Indian founder toward professional buyers

The market for "AI writing that passes" has two halves with opposite economics and a third, under-served buyer. The law nowhere bans AI writing that reads as human. It bans the evasion pitch, deception about origin, fake reviews, commercial help with assessed work, and stripped or missing labels. Read together, the evidence says: sell grounded, voice-matched writing to professionals, prove originality with receipts, and never promise evasion.

### Humanizers are a student market that every gatekeeper is closing

The funded and acquired companies in this market are detectors. Turnitin was bought for about $1.75 billion in 2019 ([Chronicle of Higher Education](https://www.chronicle.com/article/Why-a-Plagiarism-Detection/245832)); GPTZero reported $30 million in annual recurring revenue before Superhuman, the company formerly called Grammarly, announced in June 2026 that it would acquire it, according to a secondary report citing Fortune ([Crypto Briefing 2026](https://cryptobriefing.com/superhuman-acquires-gptzero-ai-detector/)); Pangram has raised about $13 million ([TechCrunch 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/29/as-ai-content-floods-the-internet-pangram-raises-9m-to-detect-it/)). Humanizers are mostly bootstrapped consumer subscriptions at $5–$20 a month on annual billing. Undetectable AI lists $9.99 a month for 10,000 words beside a counter claiming 24.5 million users ([Undetectable AI pricing](https://undetectable.ai/pricing)), and the biggest sites draw millions of visits, an estimated 9.36 million a month for Humanize AI Pro and 5.68 million for StealthWriter in June 2026 ([OneLittleWeb 2026](https://onelittleweb.com/digital-market-intelligence/ai-tools/ai-humanizers/)). Students are the core buyer: 94% of UK undergraduates use generative AI for assessed work and 12% paste AI text straight into it ([HEPI 2026](https://www.hepi.ac.uk/reports/student-generative-ai-survey-2026/)), vendors advertise help to "avoid Turnitin AI detection" ([Ryne pricing](https://ryne.ai/pricing)), and most sites lost 15–33% of their traffic between May and June 2026 as terms ended ([OneLittleWeb 2026](https://onelittleweb.com/digital-market-intelligence/ai-tools/ai-humanizers/)).

The product does not reliably do what it promises. No independent study shows a commercial humanizer beating Pangram while keeping fluent, faithful text: an NBER audit found one pushing GPTZero's miss rate to about half while Pangram stayed "largely robust" ([Jabarian & Imas 2025](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34223)), GPTZero's own paper reports the opposite ordering ([Adam et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13042)), and Turnitin publishes no accuracy for its bypasser detection. Customer complaints follow the same lines: Trustpilot pages are dominated by billing disputes, such as charges after cancellation and trials that convert to annual plans, and by output that is "still flagged" or drifts in meaning ([Trustpilot: Undetectable AI](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/undetectable.ai); [Trustpilot: StealthWriter](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/stealthwriter.ai)). The incumbents are bundling every side. Superhuman owns a writing assistant, a free humanizer, an AI detector, GPTZero and "Authorship," a process tracker that produced up to 5 million reports in a year ([NBC News 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/internet/college-students-ai-cheating-detectors-humanizers-rcna253878)); Turnitin added Clarity, its writing-process record, in May 2025 ([Plagiarism Today 2025](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/08/27/turnitin-launches-anti-ai-humanizer-feature/)); and QuillBot and Quetext give humanizing away in free tiers ([QuillBot Premium](https://quillbot.com/premium); [Quetext pricing](https://www.quetext.com/pricing)). A standalone humanizer competes with free features from the companies that also sell the detector, and its core claim expires each time the detector retrains.

### Professionals already pay for half the job

Agencies, brand teams and publishers already pay $65–$200 a month per team for one half of the problem, either detection or rewriting: Pangram Professional costs $65, Originality.ai Enterprise $179 ("Ideal for agencies & publishers"), StealthWriter Business $200 and Walter Writes Teams $99 for ten seats ([Pangram pricing](https://www.pangram.com/pricing); [Originality.ai pricing](https://originality.ai/pricing); [StealthWriter pricing](https://stealthwriter.ai/pricing); [Walter Writes pricing](https://walterwrites.ai/pricing/)). Their pain is distinctiveness and platform rules, which no classroom check addresses. In a survey of 750 senior marketing leaders, reported secondhand, 72% said AI-generated content is hurting brand distinction ([DemandScience via Substack](https://tomekchojnacki.substack.com/p/everyone-sounds-the-same)). Google's spam policy targets unoriginal scaled content "no matter how it's created" and names "synonymizing" as an obfuscation technique ([Google Search spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)); Medium gives undisclosed AI writing "Network Only" distribution and keeps AI-generated writing off its paywall ([Medium](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/22576852947223-Artificial-Intelligence-AI-content-policy)); Amazon KDP requires disclosure of AI-generated text "even if you applied substantial edits afterwards" ([KDP](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G200672390)). Freelancers and in-house copywriters are judged by detectors that a client or manager controls, and one copywriter lost 90% of his income to a client's detector despite showing timestamped Google Docs history ([Gizmodo 2024](https://gizmodo.com/ai-detectors-inaccurate-freelance-writers-fired-1851529820)). No product found sells grounded, voice-matched drafting with an originality report and a process log as its main promise, and fact-checking is sold only on the detector side, by Originality.ai ([Originality.ai pricing](https://originality.ai/pricing)). Rewriting APIs already sell at about $0.25 per 1,000 words, against $0.042–$0.072 for detection ([ZeroGPT pricing](https://www.zerogpt.com/pricing)).

### Indian demand is large, price-sensitive and thinly served

India is the second-largest country for humanizer traffic, an estimated 2.55 million visits a month or 17.7% of the category, behind the US at 34.7% ([OneLittleWeb 2026](https://onelittleweb.com/digital-market-intelligence/ai-tools/ai-humanizers/)). It also has a formal driver for similarity checks. The UGC's 2018 regulations penalize theses, dissertations and research papers above 10% similarity, up to cancelled registration above 60%, while excluding essays and assignments ([UGC Regulations 2018](https://www.ugc.gov.in/pdfnews/7771545_academic-integrity-Regulation2018.pdf)), and the national ShodhShuddhi programme has run on Bengaluru's DrillBit since October 2023 across 1,198 institutions ([INFLIBNET](https://pds.inflibnet.ac.in/)). Prices are low. DrillBit sells for ₹75,000 to ₹2.5 lakh against Turnitin's $30,000–$70,000 a year ([The Ken 2025](https://the-ken.com/story/india-made-1152-colleges-use-a-plagiarism-checker-then-chatgpt-unleashed-chaos/)), universities charge students ₹250–₹1,500 per similarity report ([Avinashilingam form](https://avinuty.ac.in/sites/avinuty.ac.in/files/2025-06/Form-2%20Plagiarism%20Checking%20Request.pdf)), and the consumer anchor is ChatGPT Go at ₹399 a month on UPI, against ₹1,999 for ChatGPT Plus ([TechCrunch 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/18/openai-launches-a-sub-5-chatgpt-plan-in-india)). No humanizer was found with official rupee pricing or UPI payments, no Indian humanizer has public traction, and reviewers in Nigeria and elsewhere already call $20 tools "expensive for those of us using it from developing countries" ([Trustpilot: WriteHuman](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/writehuman.ai)). Indian languages are a thin lane: Turnitin's AI writing report covers seven languages and its bypasser detection only English ([Turnitin Guides](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/28234943089933-File-requirements-for-an-AI-Writing-Report)), and some Indian universities offer no similarity checks at all for non-English documents ([IIS University](https://research.iisuniv.ac.in/sites/research/files/Plagiarism%20Service%20.pdf)). Taken with the finding that AI suggestions pull Indian writers toward American defaults, the opening in India is writing that keeps Indian English and Indian-language voice intact, sold to businesses at professional prices with a rupee tier for individuals; cheap humanizing for Indian students is the weakest version of the business.

### The law targets the evasion pitch and the missing label

The EU sets the hardest technical duty. Article 50(2) of the AI Act requires providers of systems that generate synthetic text to ensure outputs are "marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated," with an exception for systems that "perform an assistive function for standard editing" ([Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng)). The Commission's July 2026 guidelines settle three points that bind an Indian startup. A company offering a generative application on the EU market under its own name is a provider "regardless of whether the provider is established or located in the Union or in a third country." "Paraphrasing or rewriting text that changes style, structure and meaning beyond mere grammatical and minor stylistic correction" requires marking, which puts humanizing and voice rewriting squarely in scope, and mixed human and AI text still counts as synthetic. And a provider "may rely on the marking solution implemented by an upstream model provider" while remaining responsible for demonstrating compliance ([Commission Guidelines C(2026) 5054](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/guidelines_on_the_implementation_of_the_transparency_obligations_for_certain_ai_systems_under_article_50_of_the_ai_act_bzptwqhk0ikg1dtlddap41psfy_131215.pdf)). The duty has applied since 2 August 2026, and systems already on the market before then have until **2 December 2026** ([Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)); fines reach €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover, whichever is lower for SMEs and start-ups ([AI Act Article 99](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-99)). The voluntary Code of Practice requires watermarking of free-form text longer than 200 tokens and commits its signatories, Anthropic among them, never to "promote or advertise the use of tools whose purpose is to circumvent the machine-readable markings" ([EU Code of Practice](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129555)). The visible label under Article 50(4) is the customer's duty, and only for published text on matters of public interest. It falls away when the text "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control" and a named person holds editorial responsibility; the guidelines make fact-checking "a minimum requirement," void the exception if substantive AI edits follow the review, and leave advertising and product descriptions out of scope unless they make health, safety or sustainability claims ([Commission Guidelines C(2026) 5054](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-07/guidelines_on_the_implementation_of_the_transparency_obligations_for_certain_ai_systems_under_article_50_of_the_ai_act_bzptwqhk0ikg1dtlddap41psfy_131215.pdf)).

The pipeline consequence matters more than the fine. On Claude the mark arrives with the text, so the simplest compliant design keeps every word Claude-chosen. A second rewriting stage on a non-watermarking model weakens the upstream mark and is itself in-scope rewriting; an open-weights voice model or an Antislop-style sampler has to carry the startup's own mark for EU users, since the Code says logging alone is "not considered sufficient"; the OpenAI API needs watermarking switched on; and Gemini API coverage is unstated ([EU Code of Practice](https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/129555); [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/eu-text-provenance/)). The fine-tuning lever and the compliance shortcut therefore pull against each other, and the architecture has to choose deliberately.

India's rules are lighter for text and heavier for personal data. The amended IT Rules (G.S.R. 120(E), in force 20 February 2026) define "synthetically generated information" as "audio, visual or audio-visual information," having dropped text from the October 2025 draft, so a text-only writing product has no Indian labelling duty today ([MeitY consolidated IT Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf); [MeitY explanatory note 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/10/8e40cdd134cd92dd783a37556428c370.pdf)). Images, audio or video, such as visuals for LinkedIn posts, need a prominent label and permanent metadata with a unique identifier, and the intermediary "shall not enable the modification, suppression or removal of the label" ([MeitY consolidated IT Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/550681ab908f8afb135b0ad42816a1c9.pdf)). MeitY's February 2026 advisory treats "AI platforms, AI tool providers" as intermediaries that must prevent information that "deceives or misleads the addressee about the origin of the message," a status a lawyer should test ([MeitY advisory 2026](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2026/02/c19f3a28a6aa2128e51b0fd22d82626a.pdf)). The Digital Personal Data Protection Act's notice, consent, rights and penalty provisions start about 13–14 May 2027 ([Gazette G.S.R. 843(E)](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/c56ceae6c383460ca69577428d36828b.pdf)), with penalties up to ₹250 crore for security failures ([PIB 2025](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Writing samples and the voice profiles built from them are personal data, so they need an itemised notice, consent for that specific purpose, withdrawal as easy as consent, encryption and access logs, breach reports to the Board within 72 hours and verifiable parental consent for children ([DPDP Rules 2025](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)). The CCPA's 2022 advertising guidelines forbid exaggerating "the accuracy, scientific validity or practical usefulness" of a product, with penalties up to ₹10 lakh, or ₹50 lakh for repeat offences, per a law-firm summary ([Nishith Desai Associates](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/guidelines-on-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-notified-under-the-consumer-protection-act-2019-6172)).

Four common-law jurisdictions criminalise commercial help with assessed work, in wording that reaches AI services. In England a fee-charging provider of a "relevant service" commits an offence, advertising it to students is a separate offence, a contract term saying the material is not for submission "is not, of itself, to be taken as sufficient evidence" of a defence, and directors can be personally liable ([Skills and Post-16 Education Act 2022, s.27](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/21/section/27/enacted); [s.28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/21/section/28/enacted); [s.29](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/21/section/29/enacted)). Australia sets "2 years imprisonment or 500 penalty units, or both," applies strict liability to whether the work forms part of an assessment task, makes advertising such a service a separate offence, and has had internet providers block more than 600 sites, including four ".ai" essay-writer domains ([TEQSA Act amendment 2020](https://legislation.gov.au/C2020A00078/asmade/2020-09-03/text/original/pdf); [TEQSA blocked sites](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/blocked-illegal-cheating-websites)). Ireland criminalises both provision and advertising, with fines up to €100,000 and up to five years in prison ([Irish Qualifications Act s.43A](https://revisedacts.lawreform.ie/eli/2012/act/28/section/43A/revised/en/html); [QQI](https://www.qqi.ie/what-we-do/quality-assurance-of-education-and-training/prosecution-of-contract-cheating)), and California and New York ban selling papers for academic credit, New York as a class B misdemeanor ([California Ed. Code §66400](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=EDC&sectionNum=66400); [NY Educ. Law §213-b](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EDN/213-B)). Legal scholars argue these laws "may apply even to general-purpose AI services like chatGPT, without knowledge and intent" ([Gaumann & Veale 2024](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10190179/)). No AI or humanizer company has been prosecuted, but "bypass Turnitin" advertising is precisely the evidence of knowledge these statutes look for.

In the US the exposure is the startup's own claims. The FTC's order against Workado, whose detector claimed 98% accuracy but measured 53% on general content, requires "competent and reliable evidence to support the claim at the time it is made" ([FTC 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/04/ftc-order-requires-workado-back-artificial-intelligence-detection-claims)); "undetectable" and "100% human score" are the same kind of testable claim, made against detectors that retrain every few months. The fake-review rule makes writing a review whose reviewer does not exist a per-violation civil-penalty offence, and the FTC named "AI-generated fake reviews" when it adopted it ([16 CFR Part 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465); [FTC 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). The current Commission set aside its order against the AI writer Rytr, saying a technology should not be condemned "simply because it potentially could be used in a problematic manner" ([FTC 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-reopens-sets-aside-rytr-final-order-response-trump-administrations-ai-action-plan)), so users' misuse is a smaller risk than the startup's marketing copy.

The model vendors draw the same line from the supply side, and breaching their terms puts the startup's API access at risk. Anthropic's usage policy, in the version taking effect on 12 November 2026, forbids using Claude to "plagiarize or submit AI-assisted work without proper permission or attribution" and to "circumvent the guardrails or terms of other platforms or services" ([Anthropic Usage Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup)); OpenAI bans "academic dishonesty" and, in its consumer terms, representing "that Output was human-generated when it was not" ([OpenAI Usage Policies](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/); [OpenAI Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/)); Google bans "claiming it was created solely by a human, in order to deceive" and bars Gemini API apps "likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18" ([Google Prohibited Use Policy](https://policies.google.com/terms/generative-ai/use-policy); [Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). None of them bans text that reads as human. Distribution platforms add their own gates: Google Ads refuses "essay or thesis generators" ([Google Ads policy](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/6016086?hl=en)), Google Play bans apps that help users "deceive or mislead others" ([Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888077?hl=en)), Stripe prohibits businesses that "make outrageous claims" ([Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses)), and Apple requires explicit permission before personal data goes to third-party AI ([App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Copyright limits what "unique" can mean. US law does not protect purely AI-generated text, because "prompts do not alone provide sufficient control" ([US Copyright Office 2025](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)), and the Supreme Court declined Thaler's appeal on 2 March 2026 ([Supreme Court docket 25-449](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-449.html)); India's Copyright Act names "the person who causes the work to be created" as the author of computer-generated works, a test no court has applied to generative AI ([Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/generative-ai-and-copyright-authorship-interpreting-section-of-the-indian-copyright-act)); and Anthropic assigns its rights in outputs only "if any" ([Anthropic Commercial Terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)). "Unique" can honestly mean "not copied, with evidence"; it cannot mean "exclusive and legally protected." Elsewhere, China's labelling rules have covered text since September 2025 and ban tools that remove labels ([CAC 2025](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm)), and South Korea's AI Basic Act has required notice of generative output since January 2026, which for document-drafting services can be a logo and a notice before use, according to a law-firm summary ([Shin & Kim](https://shinkim.com/eng/media/newsletter/3190)).

### What to build, promise and price

The evidence supports one positioning: writing that is the customer's own, true and original, with the receipts. Concretely, that means a draft built from the customer's facts, opinions and samples; cited and checked sources; a similarity report from a named checker; a check against the product's other customers' output on the same topic; and an editorial log of who reviewed what, which is what EU publishers need for the Article 50(4) exception and what a freelancer needs when a client's detector fires. The buyers, in order of evidence: content and SEO agencies and brand teams in India and abroad at $99–$200 per team per month, plus an API priced near the $0.25-per-1,000-words rewriting anchor; then freelancers, job seekers and non-native professionals at $10–$20 a month or a ₹399–₹999 UPI tier; then researchers who need citation-grounded drafting with a similarity pre-check, positioned as UGC compliance and never as a way past DrillBit or Turnitin. The product should refuse to produce submittable assessed work, reviews or testimonials in a purported customer's voice, and undisclosed impersonation of real people, and it should never offer watermark removal. Its marketing should never say "undetectable," "bypass Turnitin," "0% AI," "guaranteed" or "you own the copyright"; each phrase is a deception about origin, an unsubstantiable performance claim, or both. Billing should be monthly with a visible cancel button, because auto-renewal disputes drive the largest share of one-star reviews in this market.

Three uncertainties deserve testing before heavy building. No survey measures willingness to pay for the bundle of grounding, voice and originality report, so a smoke test or customer interviews come first. Nobody has shown that a LoRA voice model trained on a typical customer's few thousand words reproduces the fine-tuning result, so the voice feature should start with samples and repair and graduate to tuning only where a customer's corpus and the measurements justify it. And three legal questions need an Indian and an EU lawyer: whether an India-incorporated SaaS selling to EU users should sign the Code of Practice, whether MeitY can treat it as an intermediary, and how far England's knowledge defence protects a general assistant that some students will misuse.

## Conclusion

The goals a founder might treat separately, writing that experts read as human, writing that is original, and a business that stays legal and platform-safe, resolve to a single engineering decision: put information and a voice the model lacks into the text, choose among divergent drafts with outside judges, and leave provenance alone. The cheap alternative, rewriting until a score drops, is being closed from every side at once, by detectors that retrain on humanizer output, by watermarks that travel with Claude's text, by EU rules that classify style rewriting as generation, by cheating statutes that make advertising such services an offence in itself, and by the vendors' own usage policies. The durable signals of human authorship are the expensive ones (first-hand specifics, a committed position, the author's own word habits), and they are the only signals no detector can learn to penalize without penalizing people, which makes them both the quality strategy and the moat.

Uniqueness also has to become a measured product property instead of a marketing word. Plagiarism checkers cannot see the sameness that matters, because model text is new at the eight-word level and redundant at the level of angle, example and metaphor, so a multi-tenant writing service inherits a check that no detector or checker performs: comparing each draft with a generic draft for the same brief and with its own other customers' output on the same topic. For an Indian founder building on Claude, the watermark turns from a constraint into a design rule. The product can never credibly promise invisibility, it inherits the EU-required mark from Anthropic as long as the words stay Claude's (it still has to document that reliance), and every open-weights voice model or rewriting stage it adds brings the marking duty back in-house. As provenance and process records replace classifier scores, with Turnitin Clarity, Superhuman's Authorship and three providers' watermarks already live, the product that lasts will be the one that can show how each piece was made and whose knowledge it contains.
