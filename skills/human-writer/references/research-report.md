# Surprise, overlap and keys decide authorship

Every detector that matters reduces to one of three measurements, and a writing style can move only the first. AI-text and AI-code detectors score how predictable a passage is to a language model and how evenly that predictability is spread: GLTR's top-10 rank buckets, GPTZero's 2023 rule that "a perplexity above 85 is more likely than not from a human source," Binoculars' perplexity-to-cross-perplexity ratio thresholded at 0.9015, the 2025–26 "alignment imprint" statistics that compare an instruction-tuned model's likelihood against its base model, and supervised products (Turnitin, GPTZero, Pangram) that learn the same surprisal structure plus the register instruction tuning imprints: nominalizations at roughly **2x** human rates, present-participial clauses at **2–5x**, evaluative vocabulary, few hedges, uniform structure. Plagiarism checkers never model style at all; they normalize text or code, hash every k-gram, keep a position-independent subset (winnowing retains about 2/(w+1) of all hashes and guarantees any shared run of w+k−1 tokens is found), and flag any run of consecutive normalized tokens that survives in an index: **8 words** in Turnitin, **9 structural tokens** in JPlag's Java front end, **39 syntax-tree tokens** in Dolos. Watermarks (Google's Gemini since 2024, Anthropic default-on worldwide since **2 August 2026** including Claude Code and the API, OpenAI in the EU from October 2026) correlate token choices with a secret key and are by construction invisible to, and independent of, every stylistic feature. Accuracy is bimodal: the best supervised text classifier reports a **0.0041% false-positive rate** on over a million human texts and an independent 2025 audit measured 0–0.1%, while zero-shot statistics invert outright when a generator samples above temperature 1 or applies a repetition penalty (Binoculars AUROC **0.002–0.07**), flagged **61%** of non-native TOEFL essays in 2023, score below chance on legal prose, and every content-based AI-code detector remains "far below practical usability" with true-positive rates under 0.3 at 1% false positives. Text matchers catch verbatim and machine-paraphrased copying at plagdet 0.88–0.90 but human summaries at only 0.56 and translation essentially never. The measurable patterns that make prose register as machine-made are, in order of evidence, lexical under-repetition with synonym cycling, the instruction-tuned grammatical register, evaluative-vocabulary density, structural uniformity, and the absence of specifics; for code they are uniform line lengths, blank-line paragraphing, exhaustive comments, spec-derived names and template-sized functions. Much folk advice backfires: banning em dashes, enforcing short sentences, avoiding passives and repetition, and injecting informality all create new, already-catalogued uniformities. Style cannot touch keyed watermarks, memorized verbatim fragments, provider-side retrieval, or the git-metadata provenance by which AI code is actually attributed in 2026.

## AI-text detectors threshold token surprise, then learn the register

### Zero-shot statistics: five ways of asking "is this too predictable?"

Every training-free detector computes some function of the per-token log-probability log p_θ(x_i | x_<i), the token's rank in the scorer's sorted next-token distribution, or the entropy of that distribution, and then thresholds it. The 2019 GLTR tool made the mechanism visible: it colours each token green if its rank under GPT-2 is ≤10, yellow if ≤100, red if ≤1,000 and purple beyond, because **human text uses words outside the top-100 2.41x as often as GPT-2 text**; the rank-bucket histogram alone reaches AUC 0.87 and raising it on screen lifted human detection accuracy from 54.2% to 72.3% ([GLTR](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.04043)). GPTZero's January 2023 launch used two document-level scalars, perplexity (exp of mean negative log-likelihood) and "burstiness" (variation of perplexity across sentences), with the only published guideline being that "a perplexity above 85 is more likely than not from a human source" ([GPTZero support](https://support.gptzero.me/hc/en-us/articles/15130070230551-How-do-I-interpret-burstiness-or-perplexity)); the most-cited open reimplementation loads GPT-2, computes per-sentence perplexity, reports burstiness as the maximum per-sentence value rather than a variance, and labels mean perplexity below 60 as AI and below 80 as "probably partly AI" ([BurhanUlTayyab/GPTZero](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/BurhanUlTayyab/GPTZero/main/model.py)). GPTZero states it stopped using these two features in autumn 2023 in favour of a proprietary deep-learning classifier ([GPTZero support](https://support.gptzero.me/hc/en-us/articles/15130070230551-How-do-I-interpret-burstiness-or-perplexity)), but the cleanest causal evidence of what the 2023 generation measured is Liang et al.'s intervention: seven commercial detectors flagged **61.22%** of 91 TOEFL essays versus 5.19% of US eighth-grade essays, the unanimously flagged essays had significantly lower GPT-2 perplexity (P = 9.74E-05), and prompting ChatGPT to "enhance word choice" cut the false-positive rate to 11.77% while simplifying the native essays raised theirs to 56.65% ([Liang et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.02819)).

The second family measures sensitivity of likelihood to local rewrites. DetectGPT scores d(x) = log p_θ(x) − E_{x̃~q(·|x)} log p_θ(x̃), estimated with k = 100 T5-3B mask-fill rewrites (15% of words masked in two-word spans) and optionally divided by the standard deviation of the perturbed log-probabilities; the hypothesis is that machine text sits near local maxima of log-probability so rewrites lose likelihood while human text does not. It reached ~0.97 AUROC on GPT-2-to-NeoX text in 2023 but costs roughly 100 generations plus 101 scorings per passage ([DetectGPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.11305)) and later fell to 0.6228 on GPT-4 text and to a below-chance 34.44 average on the 2024 DetectRL benchmark ([Fast-DetectGPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.05130); [DetectRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23746v1)). Su et al.'s cheaper variants are LRR = −Σ log p_θ(x_i|x_<i) / Σ log r_θ(x_i|x_<i), the ratio of log-likelihood to log-rank, and NPR, the ratio of mean log-rank after small perturbations to log-rank before; both run in 0.08–0.19 s per sample versus 7–10 s for DetectGPT, and log-rank proved "more discernible than the log likelihood" because rank is invariant to how peaked the distribution is at a position ([Su et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.05540)). Fast-DetectGPT replaced rewrites with the scorer's own next-token distribution: d(x) = (log p_θ(x|x) − μ̃)/σ̃ where μ̃ and σ̃² are the mean and variance of log p_θ over alternative tokens sampled independently at each position, computable analytically from one forward pass, a **340x speed-up** with AUROC 0.9887 white-box versus DetectGPT's 0.9554, 0.9615 on ChatGPT and 0.9061 on GPT-4 ([Fast-DetectGPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2310.05130)). The statistic is literally a z-score of the actual token's log-probability against what the scorer would have drawn itself, summed over positions: machine text is text whose every token is at least as likely as the scorer's own samples.

The third family compares two models. Binoculars scores B(s) = log PPL_M1(s) / log X-PPL_{M1,M2}(s), the observer's per-token negative log-likelihood divided by the mean per-token cross-entropy between an observer (Falcon-7B) and a performer (Falcon-7B-Instruct) sharing a tokenizer; the denominator estimates how surprising the text "should" be given an unknown prompt, which fixes the "capybara problem" where an unusual prompt makes machine text look high-perplexity. The reference code thresholds at **0.9015** (best F1) or **0.8536** (chosen for 0.01% FPR), labels scores below threshold as AI, truncates input to 512 tokens, and detected over 90% of ChatGPT samples at 0.01% FPR on 2024 data ([Hans et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v1); [detector.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ahans30/Binoculars/main/binoculars/detector.py)). The 2025–26 successors make the model-pair contrast explicit: LAPD computes an information-weighted alignment imprint S(x) = Σ_t I_φ(x_t)·[log P_φ(x_t) − log P_θ(x_t)] between an instruction-tuned model φ and its base θ on the theory that alignment "reallocates probability mass toward a narrower set of preferred responses, causing entropy contraction," reporting average AUROC **92.37 versus 82.26 for Fast-DetectGPT and 84.03 for Binoculars** across four benchmarks and TPR at 0.5% FPR of 92.27 versus 49.60–57.37 ([LAPD](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16923v1)); IRM uses the implicit DPO reward r(y) = Σ_i log[π_instruct(y_i|y_<i)/π_base(y_i|y_<i)], reaching 91.77% versus 87.67% for Binoculars on DetectRL with a Llama-3.2-1B pair ([IRM](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.21223v1)); DivScore divides mean token entropy under a domain-adapted model by its cross-entropy with the general model and lifts legal-text AUROC from Binoculars' 38.47 to 98.88 ([DivScore](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06705v1)).

The fourth and fifth families measure structure rather than mean likelihood. DNA-GPT truncates a text at ratio γ = 0.5–0.7, regenerates K = 10–20 continuations, and counts weighted n-gram overlap (n ≥ 4, weight n·log n) between regenerations and the original, on the "likelihood-gap hypothesis" that LLMs roughly maximise likelihood from a prefix and will regenerate the same n-grams ([DNA-GPT](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.17359)). Luminol compares perplexity before and after shuffling sentences within paragraphs and finds the ratio is "typically 4 to 5x" for machine text (mean 5.58) versus 20–60% for human text (mean 1.59), attributed to LLMs maintaining local consistency without global planning ([Luminol](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25860v2)). Lastde computes a diversity entropy over sliding windows of the token log-probability sequence because human sequences "fluctuate more abruptly" while LLM sequences are "more compact" ([Lastde](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06072v1)); FourierGPT finds larger low-frequency power in the z-scored surprisal spectrum of machine text ([FourierGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19874v1)); TOCSIN measures how much a BARTScore changes when a random 1.5% of tokens is deleted, finding LLM text has "higher token cohesiveness" ([Ma & Wang 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.16914v1)); and the persistent-homology dimension of RoBERTa token embeddings sits near **9.5 for human English and about 8 for AI text "regardless of the generator"**, with the geometric reading that human text has more degrees of freedom ([Tulchinskii et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.04723)).

Two properties of this whole family matter for a style guide. First, the signal is two-sided: Binoculars assigns random token sequences a mean score of about 1.35 versus about 1 for human text, so text that is too surprising is also non-human, and under a generator sampled at temperature 1.1 or with a repetition penalty of 1.05 Binoculars' AUROC collapses to 0.0200 and 0.0711 respectively because the output becomes "too human-like, i.e., surprising" ([Hans et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v1); [Dubois et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.609.pdf)). Second, the scorer defines the verdict: on Kazakh text Binoculars scored 0.931 AUROC with one small open scorer but 0.509 with another, and the authors conclude "the scoring model matters more than the statistic," while warning that scorers that saw the human sources during pretraining can make human text look machine-made ([Kazakh study 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.15870)).

### Supervised classifiers: probability profiles, synthetic mirrors and hard-negative mining

The first trained detector, OpenAI's RoBERTa-large fine-tuned on GPT-2 outputs versus WebText, reached "approximately 95% accuracy" on 510-token samples, accuracy that fell for nucleus-sampled text and for larger generators ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/openai-community/roberta-large-openai-detector)); by 2025 the same model labelled 90–98% of human passages as AI at every threshold ([Jabarian & Imas 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34223/w34223.pdf)). OpenAI's own 2023 classifier flagged only 26% of AI text while mislabelling 9% of human text and was withdrawn after six months "due to its low rate of accuracy" ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/openai-ai-classifier-no-longer-available-429912)). Ghostbuster shows what such classifiers actually learn: it runs each document through a unigram model, a Kneser-Ney trigram and GPT-3 ada and davinci, obtains aligned per-token probability vectors, searches 2,534 compositions of them with vector and scalar operators to depth 3, and feeds the selected features to logistic regression; in-domain F1 is 99.0, but removing the ada/davinci features drops it to 88.2 and using only seven handcrafted features gives 80.5, so **the decisive signal is the relationship between how predictable each token is under models of different strength**, not any lexical tell ([Ghostbuster](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15047v3)). RADAR co-trains a RoBERTa detector against a T5 paraphraser rewarded for fooling it and gains paraphrase robustness (0.857 versus 0.651 AUROC against an unseen GPT-3.5 paraphraser) at the cost of clean accuracy ([RADAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03838v1)).

The commercial products that disclose anything describe the same idea at scale with topic-matched negatives. Pangram's 2024 report trained a transformer on ~28M pre-2021 human documents, generated for each a "synthetic mirror" matched in topic, length and style, filtered boilerplate openers like "Sure," and "Title:", and ran hard-negative mining loops that cut held-out false positives from 2.29% to 0.25% overall and from 5.34% to 0.23% on Wikipedia ([Pangram tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v1)); Pangram 4 (July 2026) puts linear heads on an open-weight mixture-of-experts backbone, emits tokenwise human / AI-assisted / AI-generated logits, decodes with a sliding window and linear-chain CRF, trains a humanizer-detection probe with a stop-gradient because letting those gradients into the backbone "concentrated errors in academic writing and ESL essays," and reports FPR 0.0041% on over a million human texts, FNR 0.34% across 26 generators, 97.67% of 13 commercial humanizers flagged, and AI-polished human text flagged only 0.01% of the time ([Pangram 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27183)). Originality.ai says it trained an ELECTRA-style bidirectional discriminator on 160 GB of text then fine-tuned on millions of samples with varied temperature and sampling, and reports an estimated share of AI text rather than a confidence ([Originality.ai](https://originality.ai/blog/how-does-ai-content-detection-work)). Turnitin breaks a submission into overlapping segments "of text that are roughly a few hundred words (about five to ten sentences)," scores each sentence 0 (human) to 1 (entirely AI), pools multiple scores per sentence, and averages across segments into a document percentage; the minimum is 300 words of English prose, lists and bullet points are excluded, and scores under 20% carry an asterisk because of a "higher incidence of false positives" ([Turnitin FAQ](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/28477544839821-Turnitin-s-AI-writing-detection-capabilities); [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/turnitin-false-positives-AI-detector/652221/)). GPTZero's current system is "an end-to-end deep learning approach" with trinary human/mixed/AI class probabilities, a hidden-Markov sentence highlighter and an output mapping "that favors false negatives over false positives" ([GPTZero technology](https://gptzero.me/technology)); its February 2026 report names a loss L = L_d + αL_s over sentence windows and subclasses for pure AI, AI-polished and AI-paraphrased text, with all architecture details proprietary ([GPTZero report](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13042v1)).

Turnitin's VP of AI gave the most useful plain-language description of what these classifiers key on: writing that is "too consistently average," because language models pick the most probable next word, and he noted that writing with set conventions (economics, math, lab reports) can look similarly average and be falsely flagged ([Washington Post via AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/reports/2901)). Vendors that train on topic-matched mirrors cannot learn topic, so what remains is probability structure and register, which is exactly the signal that also fires on formulaic human prose.

### Where text detectors break, measured

The failure modes cluster into six, each with numbers. Decoding is the dominant confound: top-k, top-p and low temperature "make detection easier," while a repetition penalty of 1.2 cut every RAID detector by up to 32 points and random sampling plus penalty on base models is hardest of all (Fast-DetectGPT ranged from 100% on GPT-4 greedy news to 0% on MPT with sampling and penalty) ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1)); under 37 decoding configurations Binoculars went from 0.9928 at temperature 0.5 to 0.0019 at 1.3 and Fast-DetectGPT to 0.1672, with the generated text's proxy perplexity rising from 2.64 to 7,049 against 14.32 for the human texts ([Dubois et al. 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-emnlp.609.pdf)). Paraphrase splits detectors by mechanism: DIPPER cut DetectGPT from 70.3% to 4.6% detection at 1% FPR and GPTZero from 13.9% to 1.2% ([Krishna et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.13408)), yet on RAID the same paraphraser left Binoculars unchanged (+0.7) and raised Originality from 85.0 to 96.7 because commercial paraphrasers have their own learnable fingerprints, while synonym swapping dropped Binoculars by 36.1 points and homoglyph substitution collapsed Originality to 9.3 and Winston to 26.3 with only GPTZero immune ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1)). Generator drift is steady: Fast-DetectGPT fell from 0.91 on Claude-3-haiku to 0.65 on Claude-3.5-haiku and from 0.80 to 0.74 across GPT-4o versions, and "texts generated by more advanced LLMs are harder to detect in the same LLM family" ([EvoBench](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.754.pdf)); Claude-instant was the lowest-scoring generator for every zero-shot detector on DetectRL (Binoculars 55.15) ([DetectRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23746v1)); and base models are harder than chat-tuned ones (Binoculars on Mistral-base 62.3 versus Mistral-chat 91.7) ([RAID](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07940v1)).

Predictable human writers are the false-positive population. Beyond the TOEFL result, Binoculars scored **38.47 AUROC, below chance, on Australian legal text** where "highly predictable (i.e., low-entropy) human texts" erase the gap ([DivScore](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06705v1)); the US Constitution scored 0.76, on the machine side of 0.901, because memorised text is high-likelihood to any LM ([Hans et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.12070v1)); formal news was harder than social-media text ([DetectRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23746v1)); the GPT-2 RoBERTa detector flagged up to 8.69% of 14,400 real pre-ChatGPT abstracts at >50% and 10.24% of NEJM abstracts at >90%, with error rising as abstracts were shortened ([Rashidi et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10727991)); and the same detector flagged posts from autism-focused subreddits at an odds ratio of 1.246 over general ones even though those posts did not have lower perplexity, suggesting modern classifiers key on stylistic regularities beyond surprisal ([Chambers & Kelley](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14729)). A 2026 Czech replication found no non-native bias in three current detector families and no perplexity gap, concluding contemporary detectors "work effectively without relying on perplexity" ([Czech replication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05769)), and Pangram reports 0% FPR on TOEFL essays versus GPTZero's 7.7% ([Pangram tech report](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v1)), so the non-native bias is a property of surprisal-based and older classifiers, not of the 2026 frontier.

Length and mixing complete the picture. Three independent curves converge on 250–500 words for stable verdicts: Ghostbuster is unreliable at or below 100 tokens and plateaus near 500 ([Ghostbuster](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.15047v3)), Turnitin requires 300 words, OpenAI's classifier required 1,000 characters, Pangram excludes anything under 50 words ([Pangram 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27183)), and Chicago Booth measured GPTZero FPR up to 3.3% on stubs under 50 words ([Jabarian & Imas 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34223/w34223.pdf)). Expert human editing of machine text at 20–40% cut Binoculars from 83.90 to 61.24 AUROC while LLM polishing of the same text was still caught at 78–80 ([Beemo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.04032)); replacing a quarter of an LLM text with human text lowered zero-shot AUROC by 20.29% but inserting LLM text into human text only by 4.48% ([DetectRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23746v1)); Turnitin's own sentence-level false-positive rate is 4% against a document-level figure under 1% that applies only above the 20% band ([Washington Post via UMD](https://www.trails.umd.edu/news/detecting-ai-may-be-impossible-thats-a-big-problem-for-teachers)). Independent audits bracket the commercial tier: in 2023 the best of 14 tools (Turnitin) reached 76% binary accuracy and machine-paraphrased AI text was caught 15% of the time ([Weber-Wulff et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.15666v1)); in 2024 seven detectors averaged 39.5% on unmodified GPT-4/Claude 2/Bard text ([Perkins et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19148v1)); in 2025 Pangram's FPR was 0.0000–0.0010 across thresholds with FNR 0.0045–0.038, GPTZero's FPR 0.0071, Originality's 0.001–0.003, and a StealthGPT rewrite pushed GPTZero's miss rate to 0.26–0.77 while Pangram stayed at or below 0.05 ([Jabarian & Imas 2025](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34223/w34223.pdf)). GPTZero disputes that audit's API field and reports a re-run at 0.05% FPR ([GPTZero, Chicago Booth 2026](https://gptzero.me/news/chicago-booth-2026/)), and its own benchmark ranks Pangram last on humanized text at 50.2% recall ([GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/detecting-ai-humanized-text-how-gptzero-stays-ahead/)); vendor-versus-vendor numbers contradict each other and no independent test of GPTZero 4o, Pangram 4 and Turnitin's August 2025 "bypasser" model together exists as of October 2026. The theory bounds all of this: any single-sample detector satisfies AUROC ≤ 1/2 + TV − TV²/2 in the total-variation distance between machine and human text ([Sadasivan et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11156)), but with n samples the distance grows as 1 − exp(−n·I_c + o(n)), so any positive gap is detectable given enough text ([Chakraborty et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.04736)), which is why every detector improves with length and why short text is the universal blind spot.

## Code detectors read whitespace and comments because logic tokens are forced

### Why perplexity transfers poorly to code

Code's rigid grammar makes most next tokens near-deterministic: "over 70% of code tokens have an entropy of less than 1," so human and machine code both look predictable and GLTR and DetectGPT lose "around 32% in AUROC" when moved from text to code ([Ye et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16133)). Without conditioning on the task, "the entropy distributions of human-written and LLM-generated code overlap significantly" (raw entropy AUROC 55.76 on MBPP) ([ATC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06069)), and perplexity is "unsuitable for high-level programming languages": the best-case AUC on CodeNet puzzles was ~91% for C/C++ but ~64% for Python and ~73% for Ruby, with 87.81% for snippets over 50 lines versus 69.75% under 20 ([Perplexity efficacy study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.16525)). General-purpose text detectors are therefore not merely weak on code but uncalibrated: on 5,069 human Python solutions versus ChatGPT output, GPTZero scored accuracy 0.4971 with true-negative rate **0.0016** (it called essentially everything AI), DetectGPT called most code human (TPR 0.2756), and adding test cases or dead code moved the five tools in different directions ([Pan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.03676)); Suh et al. confirm five text detectors at 47.83–50.83% accuracy ([Suh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04299)), and GPTZero's recall on adversarially prompted code was 0.10 ([Droid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10583)).

### What the working zero-shot methods actually perturb

DetectCodeGPT's naturalness analysis (CodeLlama-7B, Python, temperature 0.2) found machine code has mean log-likelihood **−0.827 versus −1.658 for human code** and log-rank 0.319 versus 0.811, but the gap is concentrated in whitespace tokens (log-likelihood Δ 1.609) and comments (Δ 1.525), with keywords smallest (Δ 0.428). Its detector therefore perturbs only layout: with equal probability it inserts Poisson(3) spaces at half the candidate locations or Poisson(2) newlines after half the lines, 50 times, and scores NPR(x) minus the mean NPR of the perturbed versions; average AUROC is 0.8308 versus 0.7724 for log-rank, and in ablation newline-only perturbation gives 0.8703, space-only 0.8639, combined 0.8852 while content (MLM) perturbation gives 0.5436. Human code with "freely and randomly stylized" spacing was easiest to identify as human, and "well-structured human code" was misclassified by every method ([DetectCodeGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06461)). ATC asks the detector LLM for N = 4 task descriptions, prepends each, and computes mean entropy over code tokens excluding comments; conditioning lifts AUROC from the 50s to 93.53 (MBPP) and 92.51 (APPS) ([ATC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06069)). Ye et al. have an LLM rewrite the code and measure GraphCodeBERT cosine similarity to the original, because "differences between LLM-rewritten and original code tend to be smaller when the original code is synthetic," reaching 80–82 AUROC on Python but degrading to random once 50% of identifiers are renamed ([Ye et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16133)).

### Trained detectors learn surface regularity and collapse under shift

The stylometric lineage runs straight from Caliskan-Islam's 2015 Code Stylometry Feature Set, ~120,000 lexical, layout and AST features classified with a random forest to 98% accuracy over 250 Google Code Jam programmers, where AST node bigrams were "the most discriminating features" and layout features made up only 1% of the top 928 ([Caliskan-Islam et al. 2015](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/usenixsecurity15/sec15-paper-caliskan-islam.pdf)). The AI-versus-human reuse of that set inverts the importance ranking. Whodunit (XGBoost on 140 such features; 798 human CodeChef solutions versus 798 GPT-4 solutions, comments stripped) reaches F1 0.91 and its single most important feature is **average line length (higher means GPT-4)**, followed by lower standard deviation of line length; dropping the two "gameable" features, empty-line density and whitespace ratio, costs only 0.02 F1, and correct-only solutions score 0.86 versus 0.87 for random ones, so "the signal comes from coding style, not from errors" ([Whodunit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04013)). Oedingen et al. reach 88.48% accuracy on 31,400 Python samples with seven whitespace-only features (leading, inline and trailing whitespace, empty lines, punctuation count, max line length, indented-line count), 80.50% after Black formatting, and 98.28% with TF-IDF over tokenizer tokens (93.98% formatted), concluding "the choice of the input feature set was more important than the model"; 20 untrained humans scored 48.7% ([Oedingen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15512)). Fine-tuned code encoders reach 0.95–0.99 in-distribution (CodeGPTSensor 0.992 on Python; CoDet-M4's UniXcoder 98.65; DroidDetect 99.18), and their attribution analyses name the same cues: CoDet-M4's SHAP values show "LLMs try to make the code more structured, separating its parts with empty lines, while people often do not," and Droid's gradients point at "long, descriptive comments and detailed explanations," "consecutive empty lines and extra spaces," and punctuation ([CodeGPTSensor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04704); [CoDet-M4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13733); [Droid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10583)).

Every cross-distribution test collapses. Suh et al.'s best detector (CodeT5+ AST embeddings) fell from 82.55 F1 within distribution to 43.21 across settings, with comment removal the largest single ablation drop (−3.82) ([Suh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04299)); CoDet-M4's recall on hybrid human-edited code was 33.22% ("completely fails the task") ([CoDet-M4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13733)); single-domain Droid training transferred at 47.01 ([Droid](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10583)); CodeMirage found "TPR@FPR=1% is generally below 0.3 for all detectors," making them "impractical for real-world use" ([CodeMirage](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11059)); AICD Bench's averaged macro-F1 was 0.63 in a seen domain and language, 0.20 in an unseen domain, with Fast-DetectGPT (44.99) below a random baseline (45.73) ([AICD Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02079)); and SemEval-2026 encoders scored 95–98 on validation but **25–49 on test** (GraphCodeBERT 98.21 → 25.11) because training was 91.4% Python, while a depth-2 decision tree on two features, comment ratio and verb-comment ratio, placed in the top 15% at 67.35 and a "text-like ratio" averaged 0.07 for human code versus 0.17 for LLM code ([FMI_SU system](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04157)). The practical consequence is that no content-based AI-code detector is defensible as individual evidence; the 2026 census of 180 million repositories attributes AI code instead by bot accounts, commit-message trailers, author-name suffixes and configuration files (CLAUDE.md, .cursorrules), with trailer precision 75.0–90.5% and config-file precision 92.0%, and notes that bot-account lookup alone finds only 3.3% of Claude Code's 850,157 commits ([Repository census 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24429)).

### What AI code measurably looks like

The "AI accent" in code is regularity along five axes, and the best-replicated axis is token predictability that survives naming. On 787,562 function pairs across Python, Java and C, replacing identifiers and literals with placeholders leaves human code with the highest n-gram cross-entropy in every language (Python human ≈0.95 bits/token versus 0.82 for ChatGPT and Qwen and 0.75 for DeepSeek-Coder), hierarchical clustering's top-level split separates human from all LLM authors, and after controlling for size "complexity metrics carry little signal, while naturalness separates authors"; LLM functions have "roughly half the lines, half the cyclomatic complexity, and a third to a half of the Halstead volume" ([Improta et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12708)). AI corpora have 7,756 fewer unique tokens and 63.74 fewer tokens per function; Java AI function names run 18.92–19.32 characters versus 13.98 for humans; unused-argument findings reach 32,992 (ChatGPT) to 213,264 (Qwen) per ~285k functions; and AI code carries more hard-coded prints and markedly more injection flaws (CWE-78: 13,419 for ChatGPT versus 2,243 human) ([Cotroneo et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21634)). A Java practitioner study bands mean negative log-likelihood of comment-stripped method bodies at 0.48–0.72 for humans with natural comments, 0.31–0.47 for compact competitive style, 0.22–0.34 for GPT-4o and 0.28–0.44 for Copilot with strong user style, describing human code as "bursty... short runs of very ordinary tokens followed by a single jarring choice" and warning that terse experienced coders "can look model-like" ([Codequiry blog](https://codequiry.com/blog/perplexity-thresholds-for-detecting-ai-code)).

Comments are the second axis and the most-cited. A 2026 readability study of 5,869 Python pairs finds **24 redundant comments in LLM-bad code versus 3 in human-bad code**, deficient comments 60 (human) versus 10 (LLM), inconsistent style 58 versus 15, a novel LLM-only "overblanking" pattern of meaningless blank lines, and LLM code winning readability 67.52% of the time while human code has a tighter interquartile range ([Readability Spectrum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13280)); comments are also the strongest cue for telling LLMs apart from each other (−7.2 points when removed) ([LLM-AuthorBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17323)). The counter-evidence matters for a guide: on 72 complex tasks 85% of GPT-4 outputs lacked complete docstrings versus 78% of human solutions, GPT-4 had more Pylint naming violations (72 versus 65) and 61% higher average complexity ([Jury et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16857)), and Atlassian's GPT-4 agent versus human code showed a comment-ratio difference with negligible effect size (Cliff's δ = 0.124) ([HULA study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11264)). So the robust claim is not "AI documents more" but "AI comments restate the obvious and does so uniformly." The remaining axes are idiom and context: models use built-ins where the repository has helpers (6.6% of cases), "rarely use Pythonic idioms," call deprecated APIs, show blank-line inconsistencies in 99.2% of samples against ground truth, and 66–90% of functionally correct samples differ in style from the human reference ([Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00456)); at repository scale 2024 saw an eight-fold increase in duplicated five-line-plus blocks and copy-pasted lines exceeding moved lines for the first time ([GitClear via LeadDev](https://leaddev.com/software-quality/how-ai-generated-code-accelerates-technical-debt)), near-identical insecure templates recur across unrelated projects ([AI Code in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18567)), and agentic pull requests touch fewer files, delete less and split into ~18 commits versus ~5 ([AIDev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17581)). The folk claims that fail measurement are that AI leaves no TODOs or debug prints (agents authored 525 self-admitted-debt comments and the SystemPrintln counts above are AI's) and that only humans write inconsistent style; what the data support is that humans vary within a file and encode project context, and detectors are, in effect, variance detectors.

## Plagiarism checkers count surviving k-gram runs after normalization

### The fingerprinting skeleton every text matcher shares

The winnowing paper fixed the architecture that MOSS, Turnitin's visible behaviour, Dolos and Compare50 all follow: transform the document to remove differences that should not matter ("whitespace and punctuation are removed, all letters are converted to lower case," or identifiers replaced by one symbol), cut it into k-grams, hash each, keep a position-independent subset as fingerprints, and index fingerprint → (document, offset). The noise threshold k is chosen so "common idioms of the language have length shorter than k," on the empirical observation of "a sharp threshold" between uninteresting and interesting match lengths ([Schleimer, Wilkerson & Aiken 2003](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/publications/papers/sigmod03.pdf)). Hashing is Karp–Rabin: H(c₁…c_k) = c₁·b^(k−1) + c₂·b^(k−2) + … + c_k, updated by H(c₂…c_{k+1}) = (H(c₁…c_k) − c₁·b^(k−1))·b + c_{k+1} modulo the word size, which is over four times faster than rehashing from scratch at k = 50. Selection is winnowing: with guarantee threshold t and window w = t − k + 1, "in each window select the minimum hash value," rightmost on ties, so that "at least one k-gram in any shared substring of length at least w + k − 1" is detected while the expected density is **d = 2/(w+1)**, within 33% of the proven 1.5/(w+1) lower bound for any local algorithm; robust winnowing prefers the hash the previous window chose, which fixes low-entropy strings and is what MOSS runs. The alternative "0 mod p" selection leaves unbounded gaps (a 29,900-character run with no selected hash was found in 500,000 web pages), and fixed-size sketches such as the n smallest hashes detect only whole-document resemblance, not a paragraph contained in a book ([Schleimer et al. 2003](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/publications/papers/sigmod03.pdf)). Broder's shingling formalises the two quantities, resemblance r = |S(A)∩S(B)|/|S(A)∪S(B)| and containment c = |S(A)∩S(B)|/|S(A)|, and AltaVista's 1996 production used 10-word shingles, 40-bit Rabin fingerprints, modulus-25 sampling and a 50% resemblance cutoff over 30 million pages ([Broder et al. 1997](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www6/proceedings/Technical/Paper205/Paper205.html)). For web scale, MinHash signatures of ~100 permutations estimate Jaccard directly and LSH banding with b bands of r rows makes a pair a candidate with probability 1 − (1 − s^r)^b, threshold ≈ (1/b)^(1/r), so b = 20, r = 5 catches 99.96% of 80%-similar pairs ([MMDS ch. 3](http://infolab.stanford.edu/~ullman/mmds/ch3.pdf)); Google's near-duplicate crawler used 64-bit SimHash fingerprints, where weighted feature hashes vote on each bit, and declared duplicates at Hamming distance k = 3 across 8 billion pages with precision and recall near 0.75 ([Manku et al. 2007](https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/33026.pdf)). Fingerprints find candidate documents; precise passage boundaries come from a second pass (suffix arrays, Greedy String Tiling, or dynamic programming) that merges consecutive matches into tiles tolerant of block moves.

### What Turnitin and its competitors index, floor and report

Turnitin compares against three pools: a proprietary crawl of "70 billion current and archived internet pages" that retains historical copies, a student-paper repository that stood at 1.9 billion submissions by June 2025, and licensed publisher content (69 million articles from 47,000 journals plus Crossref-gated full text) ([Turnitin blog 2019](https://www.turnitin.co.uk/blog/how-much-do-you-know-about-turnitins-content); [Wikipedia: Turnitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turnitin)). The Similarity Score "represents the percentage of your writing that is similar to something found" in those pools; Turnitin states "Similarity does not mean that your work is plagiarized," that there is no fixed acceptable score, and that zero similarity "means that you aren't including any source-based evidence" ([Turnitin student guide](https://help.turnitin.com/Resources/PDF/understanding_the_turnitin_similarity_report-a_student_guide.pdf)). The operative threshold is the word floor: small-match exclusion "defaults to 8 words, and only matches 8 words or longer appear in the Similarity Report," and 8 "is the minimum allowed value," with legacy guidance recommending 10–16 ([Turnitin Guides](https://guides.turnitin.com/hc/en-us/articles/23539146689549)); iThenticate's floor is nine words ([iThenticate filters](https://fa-help.turnitin.com/ithenticate/individual/user/the-similarity-report/filters-and-exclusions.htm)). Competitors set floors an order of magnitude lower: Unicheck "is able to detect even 4-word similarities" ([Moodle plugin](https://moodle.org/plugins/plagiarism_unicheck)), PlagScan reportedly "at least three consecutive words" ([Wikipedia: PlagScan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlagScan)), PlagAware defaults to a 50-character minimal phrase ([PlagAware](https://www.plagaware.com/sensitivity)), and Copyscape, which "uses Google as a search provider," reports pages matching at least 1–4 sentences ([Copyscape FAQ](https://www.copyscape.com/faqs.php)). Copyleaks is the only vendor with a documented three-level model, identical, minor changes (swapped articles, tense shifts) and paraphrased "related meaning," plus cross-language matching, but publishes no thresholds or accuracy ([Copyleaks docs](https://docs.copyleaks.com/concepts/features/detection-levels.md)). Technical disguises are solved at the normalisation layer: Turnitin maps lookalike characters "back to the equivalent Latin alphabet characters before running the similarity report" and flags hidden white-on-white text and hidden quotation marks as integrity warnings ([Turnitin flags](https://help.turnitin.com/feedback-studio/flags.htm)). Institutional practice codifies what vendors refuse to: the University of Ghana caps overall similarity at 20% with 2% from any single source and a 10-word limit on unbroken strings ([UG guidelines](https://sgs.ug.edu.gh/sites/sgs.ug.edu.gh/files/2023-07/%20UGTurnitinUsageGuidelines.pdf)), Kenyatta University at 16% overall and 1% per source, rejecting anything under 6% ([Kenyatta University](https://library.ku.ac.ke/?p=3151)), and Springer Nature tells editors "it is more important to look at the individual scores of the sources than the overall similarity index" ([Springer Nature](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-cms/rest/v1/content/16185086/data/v3)). Because quotes and bibliographies are usually excluded, a passage is flagged as copied precisely when it is at least the word floor long, outside quotation marks and references, present in that vendor's index, and contiguous enough to survive filtering, with no step that assesses intent, attribution or meaning.

### Paraphrase, translation and the semantic frontier

The PAN text-alignment competitions (2009–2015) fixed the paraphrase-tolerant pipeline of seeding, extension and filtering with concrete parameters. Seeds are small matches designed to survive specific edits: sorted word 3-grams and skip-grams defeat reordering, stop-word 8-grams survive synonym replacement of content words, named-entity 5-grams anchor on names, and sentence pairs whose tf-idf cosine and Dice both exceed 0.33 tolerate roughly two-thirds of the vocabulary changing ([PAN 2013](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1179/CLEF2013wn-PAN-PotthastEt2013.pdf); [PAN 2014](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1180/CLEF2014wn-Pan-PotthastEt2014.pdf)). Extension merges seeds whose gap is below 200–4,000 characters (Suchomel's rule: merge passages when the gap holds at least one seed per 10,000 characters and the combined size exceeds twice the gap), and filtering discards aligned passages shorter than 150–300 characters or 15–40 words. The 2014 winner scored plagdet 0.900 on verbatim, **0.884 on random obfuscation, 0.887 on cyclic machine translation, but 0.561 on human summaries**; the trivial baseline scored 0.934 on verbatim and 0.045 on summaries; and precision is "less of a problem compared to achieving a high recall" ([PAN 2014](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1180/CLEF2014wn-Pan-PotthastEt2014.pdf)). Embedding methods land where heavy paraphrase lives: under more than 40% synonym substitution SBERT cosine between original and rewrite stays at 0.35–0.55 ([arXiv 2512.10435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10435)), and a production-style pipeline (BM25 top-10, PAN alignment, RoBERTa paraphrase classifier accepted at 0.5–0.99 with exact named-entity match) achieves precision/recall of 0.995/0.986 on verbatim, 1.00/0.723 on paraphrase and 1.00/0.412 on idea reuse, "high-precision and low-recall" ([Lee et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.07618)). Cross-language detection is best served by character 3-grams for related languages (F1 0.962 at threshold 0.087 in a balanced test) and explicit semantic analysis otherwise ([Ferrero et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08828); [Potthast et al. 2011](https://webis.de/downloads/publications/papers/potthast_2011a.pdf)), yet in the 2018–19 TeSToP test of 15 commercial systems across eight languages "all the systems were unable to detect translation plagiarism, with the exception of Akademia," synonym replacement caused "a considerable drop" in all but Urkund, PlagiarismCheck.org and Turnitin, "none of the systems was able to provide satisfactory results" on manual paraphrase, no system rated "useful," and the testers recommended vendors "lose the single number" because percentages "may even be misleading" ([Foltýnek et al. 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40979-020-00061-1)). Citation-order analysis catches what text matching cannot, 13 of 16 translated sections in the Guttenberg thesis that text tools missed entirely ([Gipp & Meuschke 2011](https://smartlib.umri.ac.id/assets/uploads/files/9af07-gipp11c.pdf)). Intrinsic detection, which looks for style shifts inside one document using character-3-gram profiles over 1,000-letter windows and flags windows whose dissimilarity exceeds the recomputed mean plus two standard deviations, reaches recall ≈0.46 at precision ≈0.22 on PAN-09 ([Stamatatos 2009](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-502/paper8.pdf)), and even transformer-based style-change systems fall from F1 0.984 to 0.821 when every paragraph shares a topic ([PAN 2023](https://dbis-web1.uibk.ac.at/sites/default/files/2023-11/zangerle-pan-2023.pdf)); it is triage, not evidence.

### LLM memorization as a plagiarism exposure

The same pipeline applied to 10,000 unprompted GPT-2 generations found verbatim training-data passages of at least 256 characters in **0.47% (temperature) to 0.7% (top-p)** of outputs, paraphrase in 0.16–0.32% and idea reuse in 0.05–0.38%, with larger models and top-k/top-p decoding plagiarizing more and a biomedical fine-tune raising paraphrase reuse of its fine-tuning set to 5.48% ([Lee et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.07618)). Carlini et al. show GPT-J 6B can reproduce "at least 1%" of The Pile given 50-token prompts, that memorization grows log-linearly with model size (a tenfold size increase adds about 19 percentage points), duplication count and context length, and that deduplication cut memorization of rarely repeated sequences from 3.6% to 1.2% ([Carlini et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2202.07646)). The practical exposure for a writer is therefore not the document percentage (150 ChatGPT-3.5 essays averaged 22% Turnitin similarity against 33% for 150 human essays ([Revista eCiencias](https://revistas.ucr.ac.cr/index.php/reciencias/article/view/11700))) but sentence-length verbatim fragments of heavily duplicated text (encyclopaedic definitions, famous passages, boilerplate, syndicated news), which clear an 8-word floor and sit in every web index; the worst case is prompting with the opening of a known text, the method behind the New York Times' Exhibit J, which OpenAI characterised as "a rare bug" that "becomes more likely when the same content shows up many times in training data" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/01/08/openai-blog-post-new-york-times-lawsuit-not-full-story-copyright)).

### Code similarity: structural token streams, not text

Source-code tools apply the same skeleton to a normalized token stream, and the normalization is what decides what counts. MOSS runs a per-language front end that strips whitespace and comments and makes identifiers interchangeable (the authors found that "replacing all of the parameters with a single constant and increasing k by 1 worked just as well" as true parameterized matching), fingerprints with robust winnowing, discards fingerprints found in an instructor-supplied base file (-b) or in more than -m = 10 submissions, ranks pairs by fingerprints shared, and reports approximate lines matched and a per-file percentage that Stanford says "are certainly not a proof of plagiarism" ([Schleimer et al. 2003](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/publications/papers/sigmod03.pdf); [moss script](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~rjmh/Wash/moss); [MOSS home](https://theory.stanford.edu/~aiken/moss/)). JPlag parses programs into structural tokens (BeginClass, VarDef, Assign, Apply, BeginWhile, EndWhile, Return…) that ignore "whitespace, comments, and the names of identifiers" and all expression-internal structure, then covers one stream with the longest non-overlapping common substrings of the other using Greedy String Tiling accelerated by Karp–Rabin hashes of length M; similarity is 2·coverage/(|A| + |B|), the default minimum match M was 9 because "low minimum match lengths tend to produce spurious matches," and current per-language defaults are **Java 9, C/C++/Python/TypeScript 12, C#/Go/Kotlin/Rust/Swift 8, LLVM IR 70, natural text 5** ([Prechelt et al. 2002](https://www.jucs.org/jucs_8_11/finding_plagiarisms_among_a/Prechelt_L.pdf); [JPlag JavaLanguage.java](https://github.com/jplag/JPlag/blob/main/languages/java/src/main/java/de/jplag/java/JavaLanguage.java); [JPlagComparison.java](https://github.com/jplag/JPlag/blob/main/core/src/main/java/de/jplag/JPlagComparison.java)). Dolos serializes tree-sitter syntax trees so that "identifiers, string content, and comments are masked," winnows k-grams with defaults **k = 23 and window 17**, so any shared run of 39 syntax-tree tokens is guaranteed to match and runs under 23 never can, and reports similarity (fraction of shared fingerprints), total overlap and longest fragment, with a plagiarism threshold inferred from the intersection of two Gaussians fitted to the nearest-neighbour similarity histogram ([Dolos algorithm](https://github.com/dodona-edu/dolos/blob/main/docs/about/algorithm.md); [options.ts](https://github.com/dodona-edu/dolos/blob/main/lib/src/lib/options.ts); [Maertens et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10853)). Compare50 uses k = 25, t = 35, replaces identifiers with "v" and literals with their types, and weights each shared fingerprint by 1 + log(N/(1 + frequency)) so common snippets count less ([compare50 passes.py](https://github.com/cs50/compare50/blob/main/compare50/passes.py)). Clone-detection research names the granularity: Type-1 (whitespace/comments), Type-2 (identifiers/literals/types), Type-3 (statements added or changed) and Type-4 (same computation, different syntax); token-based SourcererCC with bag-of-tokens overlap ≥ 0.7·max size recalls 100%/98%/93%/61%/5%/0% across T1/T2/VST3/ST3/MT3/WT3-T4 ([SourcererCC](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.06448)), CloneDR hashes AST subtrees ignoring identifiers and scores Similarity = 2S/(2S + L + R) ([Baxter et al. 1998](https://www.semanticdesigns.com/Company/Publications/ICSM98.pdf)), and even GPT-4 recalls only 0.15–0.33 of Type-4 clones ([Dou et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.01191)).

Obfuscation results follow directly from what the token stream retains. Of the attacks real students tried against JPlag, formatting, comments, renaming, declaration splitting, modifiers and constants were "futile" because they do not change the token list; control-structure swaps, temporary variables, inlining and statement reordering caused "local confusion" by splitting a minimum-length match, but informed attackers escaped "in less than 10 percent of all cases" ([Prechelt et al. 2002](https://www.jucs.org/jucs_8_11/finding_plagiarisms_among_a/Prechelt_L.pdf)). Automated dead-code insertion (Mossad, which keeps only insertions that leave -O3 object code unchanged) drove MOSS to 15.4% average similarity against a 26.2% baseline for legitimate pairs and JPlag to 1.1%, while 30 CS PhD teaching assistants rated the result as readable as authentic code ([Mossad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01700)); JPlag's 2024 defenses, token-sequence normalization (dead-node removal plus topological re-sorting) and subsequence match merging, raise the median similarity difference on insertion attacks by 61–93 points and make a Mossad run take 110–190 minutes while code size triples ([Sağlam & Schmid 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20158)). Graph-based (program-dependence-graph) comparison is the most robust in every study that includes it but is not deployed in mainstream tools ([Cheers et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03997)). Scores are only meaningful relative to the class: honest solutions to small assignments routinely score 25–30% in MOSS and JPlag ([Mossad](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.01700)) while unrelated human pairs on larger projects have a JPlag median of 0.00–6.06%, and GPT-4 solutions to the same task cluster at a median **20.10–31.33%** against each other ([Sağlam & Schmid 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20158)); on 9,000 solutions to constrained algorithmic tasks GPT-5.2 and Gemini 3.0 Flash output showed ">75% similarity compared to 28% for humans" ([Gacek et al. 2026](https://badap.agh.edu.pl/publikacja/169730)). A single LLM-written submission does not trip MOSS or JPlag, which have no corpus of model output; convergence across many users of the same model on the same small task does. Copilot's public-code filter is a different thing again, an exact-match check of each suggestion plus about 150 characters of context against an index of public GitHub code that fires in "less than one percent" of suggestions and only for accepted, unmodified inline completions ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/completions/code-referencing)).

## Watermarks and provenance are keyed, orthogonal to style, and default-on for Claude

### Green lists, tournaments and entropy budgets

Kirchenbauer et al.'s scheme hashes the previous token to seed a pseudorandom split of the vocabulary into a green list of size γ|V| and a red list, adds δ to each green logit before softmax, and detects with a one-proportion z-test that needs only the hash function and key, not the model: z = (|s|_G − γT)/√(Tγ(1−γ)), rejected at z > 4 for a nominal one-sided false-positive rate of about 3×10⁻⁵. At γ = 0.25 and T = 200 that means at least 75 green tokens (37.5%) where human text sits near 25%; the reference implementation recommends γ = 0.25, δ = 2.0, a four-token context and the self-hash seeding scheme, and always ignoring repeated n-grams because repetitive human text otherwise produces misleading p-values ([Kirchenbauer et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.10226); [lm-watermarking](https://github.com/jwkirchenbauer/lm-watermarking)). The scheme is distortionary: oracle perplexity rises from 5.1 to 6.2 at δ = 2 and to 9–11 at δ = 5 under multinomial sampling, although beam search keeps it near 1.2 at every δ, and a soft watermark "has very little impact on the perplexity of tokens with extremely high or low entropy" ([Kirchenbauer et al. 2023 PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.10226)). The follow-up shows the signal weakened but not erased by rewriting: ROC-AUC stays above 0.85 under GPT-3.5 and DIPPER paraphrase at 200 tokens and above 0.9 at 600, and 14 graduate students asked to remove the watermark by hand were detected "after about 800 tokens" on average at a 10⁻⁵ false-positive rate ([Kirchenbauer et al. 2023b](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04634)). Google's SynthID-Text seeds g-values from a hash of the previous H = 4 tokens plus the key, draws 2^m candidates and runs m = 30 layers of pairwise tournaments in which the higher g-value wins; the non-distortionary configuration (two competitors per match, repeated contexts not re-watermarked) left thumbs-up and thumbs-down rates unchanged within 0.01–0.02% across about 20 million live Gemini responses, detection averages the g-values the observed tokens receive under the key, and the method "is less effective when there is less entropy," which lower temperature, larger models and RLHF all reduce ([Dathathri et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11499265/)). OpenAI's textGrain, published 5 October 2026, partitions the vocabulary into keyed blocks, couples the block distribution to m equally likely columns through a KL-regularised optimal-transport problem capped by an entropy budget β, and samples within the chosen block with original relative probabilities so that averaging over the key "recovers the NTP distribution"; detection reconstructs a per-token score whose sum is Gamma(n, 1) under the null and scores only the first occurrence of each context window ([textGrain report](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/e9508624-d767-41b6-a26d-e34ca798ada6/textgrain-entropy-calibrated-watermarking-for-language-model-text.pdf)); in a 400-token evaluation, replacing 10% of words with synonyms cut detection from about 92% to 66% and 25% replacement to 17%, detection is harder in mathematics, and the detector is calibrated to a 1% false-positive target ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/05/openai-details-new-text-watermarking-system-for-chatgpt-codex-and-the-api/)). The unbiased family (Aaronson's Gumbel rule, Kuditipudi's EXP decoder argmax ξ_i^{1/μ(i)}, Christ–Gunn–Zamir's cryptographically undetectable construction) keeps the marginal output distribution exactly equal to the model's while remaining detectable from about 35 tokens even after 40–50% random edits ([Kuditipudi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15593); [Christ, Gunn & Zamir](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09194)).

### What removes a watermark, and what does not

The attack literature converges on one asymmetry: edits that leave most tokens and their keyed context windows intact leave the watermark detectable, and transformations that regenerate most tokens remove it. DIPPER cut the KGW watermark from 100% to 57.2% detection at 1% FPR ([Krishna et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13408)); recursive paraphrase drove it below 20% after five rounds ([Sadasivan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156)); a quality-constrained random-walk attack moved z-scores from 6.2 to 1.6 (KGW), 8.2 to 1.5 (Unigram) and 3.5 to 0.7 (EXP) with near-neutral judged quality, leading its authors to be "not optimistic about the feasibility of strong watermarking schemes" ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04378)); querying a watermarked API enables both scrubbing and spoofing "with under $50" at "over 80%" success ([Jovanović et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19361)); watermark presence itself can be inferred black-box for under $3 ([Gloaguen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.20777)); and fine-tuning on watermarked text leaves "radioactive" traces at p < 10⁻⁵ with only 5% contamination, which a 2026 attack uses to spoof a victim's mark ([Sander et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14904)). Code is the hardest medium because entropy is low and "just one wrong token" breaks a program: SWEET watermarks and scores only tokens whose entropy exceeds a threshold τ and its AUROC "drop[s] to about 0.8 when all variables are renamed" because identifiers are most of the high-entropy positions ([SWEET](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15060)); renaming, dead-code insertion and try/catch wrapping cut KGW's TPR on Llama-7B HumanEval code from 0.79 to 0.57, 0.38 and 0.22 and Unigram's from 0.76 to 0.20, 0.01 and 0.11 ([Suresh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17983)); the AST-transformation alternative ACW survives renaming (TPR 94.76%) but falls to 71.56% under an adaptive recovery model ([ACW](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07518)). Anthropic states plainly that watermark density is lower for "factual text, exact-output contexts, code, and proofreading, where few word choices exist" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)).

### Deployment as of October 2026 and the two things this means for a Claude skill

Three frontier providers now watermark text. Google has watermarked Gemini app responses since 2024, with API coverage confirmed only by a reversed forum answer ([Google AI Developers Forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/does-gemini-api-text-output-carry-synthid-watermarking-gemini-2-5-flash-lite-gemini-3-1-flash-lite-eu-ai-act-art-50-2/177241)). Anthropic marks output of all Claude models launched from **2 August 2026, "wherever Claude is offered, worldwide,"** across Claude, the Platform API, Claude Code, Cowork and Tag; a positive result means content "may have been generated or processed by Claude" and is "not fully conclusive," the mark "will travel with the text when it's copied and pasted elsewhere, and may persist through some editing" but may be lost if "heavily edited, paraphrased, translated, or mixed with other writing," detection is in private preview for regulators, media, researchers and educational organizations, and the mechanism follows the SynthID-Text paper and Aaronson's proposal, replacing the sampler's random number generator with one determined by "the key and a few words that come before" ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)). OpenAI is rolling textGrain out to ChatGPT and Codex in the EU with a worldwide API opt-in and researcher-only detector access ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/)). No text watermark is confirmed for Microsoft Copilot, Llama, Mistral or Grok; every deployed detector is key-gated, so GPTZero reported it "was unable to find a way to detect SynthID in Gemini" ([GPTZero](https://gptzero.me/news/google-watermarking-synthid/)); and the EU AI Act's Article 50 obligations apply from 2 August 2026 with cross-provider detector interoperability required by **2 February 2027**, free-form text exempted from the two-technique rule because it "cannot carry metadata," and text under 200 tokens exempt entirely ([Paul, Weiss memo](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content)). The non-watermark channels are narrower still: Krishna et al.'s retrieval defence, in which the provider stores every generation and answers queries by BM25 nearest-neighbour search, recovers 97.3% of PG19 and 80.4% of Wikipedia paraphrases at 1% FPR over a 15-million-generation corpus, but only for that provider's closed model, with privacy costs no provider has accepted in production ([Krishna et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13408)); C2PA defines signed-manifest embeddings for images, audio, video, PDF, fonts and ZIP-based office formats but none for plain text, Markdown or source code ([C2PA specification](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.2/specs/C2PA_Specification.html)).

Two consequences follow for a Claude skill. First, a watermark positive and a stylometric verdict are independent evidence channels with uncorrelated errors: distortion-free schemes change the output distribution by definition not at all on average over keys, even the distortionary KGW scheme moves perplexity only from 5.1 to 6.2, Anthropic says its watermark "does not push the model toward unusual words it wouldn't otherwise choose," and no stylistic feature a classifier scores carries information about the key. "Humanising" style therefore leaves the watermark intact, and a full regeneration by a non-watermarking model removes the watermark regardless of how AI-like the result sounds ([Kuditipudi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15593); [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark)). Second, a watermark negative is uninformative about style or authorship, being consistent with human text, non-watermarking models, heavily edited watermarked text, short or low-entropy text, and pre-watermark output; Anthropic and OpenAI both phrase positives as "generated or processed" precisely because editing or translating human text with the model also marks it, while proofreading "mostly preserves human words, so the watermark may be too sparse to detect" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/10/05/openai-details-new-text-watermarking-system-for-chatgpt-codex-and-the-api/)). The skill's remit is therefore style and originality; the provenance channel exists by design and is outside its reach.

### Advice that backfires

The evidence base is unusually clear about rules that create a new uniformity or push text toward the machine distribution, and several are canonical style advice. "Vary your sentence length" targets a metric that is chance-level (coefficient of variation AUROC 0.521, with machine text slightly *more* variable) and a deficit, rhythm flatness, that "prompting moves without targeting": across nine model families, few-shot exemplars of human rhythm produced the *flattest* output in seven ([Opace 2026](https://opace.agency/tools/ai/content-verification-integrity/research/ai-writing-myths-measured/); [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/164820)); rhythm has to come from content-driven structure such as asides, fragments, questions and quotations, which also moves the discourse-level tells. "Never repeat a word; vary your synonyms" is the single most damaging rule for current models, since under-repetition is now the strongest measured feature and Wikipedia files "elegant variation" under historical AI signs ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)); humans re-use the same content word across adjacent sentences about three times as often. "Prefer the active voice" moves text toward GPT-4o, which uses agentless passives at half the human rate, and the passive is an inverted feature (Cliff's δ = −0.202 toward human) ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v1); [Opace 2026](https://opace.agency/tools/ai/content-verification-integrity/research/ai-writing-myths-measured/)). "Cut hedges and qualifiers; be confident" strips exactly the modals, epistemic markers, hedges, boosters and attitude markers that students use more than ChatGPT ([Herbold et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.14276); [Jiang & Hyland 2025](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/99123)); the nuance is that GPT-4o already over-uses downtoners (118–155% of human) while under-using amplifiers (46–63%), so the human direction is "very," "really," committed superlatives and definitive statements rather than "somewhat" ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v1); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)).

Punctuation bans are the clearest over-correction artifact. OpenAI's suppression of the em dash (GPT-4.1 at 10.62 per 1,000 words to GPT-5.4 at 1.43) produced the mid-2026 sign of "much more period and comma usage and shorter sentences around a strict 8–20 word limit," and Wikipedia records that newer models "appear hard-coded not to use them, so the overall variety has dropped significantly" ([Freeburg 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27006); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)). Claude models show "high unconstrained rates but excellent suppression compliance" (Opus 4.6 from 9.09 to 0.19 under a no-markdown instruction), so a Claude skill that bans dashes reproduces the GPT-5.x artifact; the human baseline is a mean of 3.23 per 1,000 words with a range of 0.33–17.12, with dashes used where humans use them rather than "in a formulaic, pat way... over-emphasizing clauses or parallelisms," and human journalists use more commas, semicolons and parentheses than any model ([Freeburg 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.27006); [Economist via The Outpost](https://theoutpost.ai/news-story/ai-writing-s-biggest-giveaway-has-changed-the-economist-report-reveals-new-detection-patterns-29372/)). Word blacklists have a half-life of about one model generation and miss the mechanism: synonym groups rise together, so replacing "delve" with "explore" leaves the density of evaluation unchanged ([arXiv 2506.21817](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21817v1)). "Always summarise at the end" and "cover both sides" are catalogued tells (optimistic restating conclusions; humans "end more abruptly") ([Russell et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v2)), and heavy markdown scaffolding (title-case headings, bold-label bullets, "key takeaways") is both a sign and a confound that inflates apparent rhythm variety ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); [NeurIPS 2026](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/164820)).

Informality and imperfection are the trap in the other direction. Contractions, first person, family detail and warmth are the cues lay readers over-trust, and they are wrong: contractions read as human (odds ratio 0.947) but were actually more common in AI profiles (1.134), first-person pronouns and family words were non-diagnostic, and classifier-selected AI profiles were rated human 65.7% of the time against 51.7% for real humans, which is why the authors warn against "informal and colloquial speech" as a humanity cue ([Jakesch et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07271)). Expert detectors are not fooled: a humanized o1-pro set was identified 100% by expert majority vote and 90% by Pangram while GPTZero fell to 46.7%, and the one expert who relied on informality cues scored 0% on it ([Russell et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15654v2)). Humanizer traces are now an explicit training target: Turnitin's August 2025 model was trained on "the signals and patterns of leading humanizers," which "remove stock AI phrases and punctuation like em dashes" yet "leave a statistical trace" ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/08/27/turnitin-launches-anti-ai-humanizer-feature/)); Pangram 4's humanizer head reaches 96.82% accuracy ([Pangram 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27183)); and a Grammarly "improve" rewrite turns 100%-human text into 100%-AI under detectors while spelling fixes have marginal effect ([Bloomberg](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~pstone/Courses/309fall24/preclass/readings/week13__Do_AI_Detectors_Work__Students_Face_False_Cheating_Accusations___Bloomberg.pdf)). Deliberate errors are not a lever for an assistant; the usable form of imperfection is structural unevenness, a one-sentence paragraph, an unfinished thread, an abrupt close, and hedging that varies with how sure the writer actually is. Formality cuts both ways and should be register-matched rather than maximised: Wikipedia lists "fancy," academic or formal prose and perfect grammar as ineffective indicators for supervised detection, but formal human writing is exactly what surprisal-based detectors flag (legal text below chance, structured abstracts at 10% false positives), so the target is the genre's own human conventions, which is also where genre dominates style in the RAID corpus ("machine poetry looks more like human poetry than like machine scientific abstracts") ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing); [RAID stylistic 2026](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.14111)).

The same logic governs code. "Document every function," "separate every logical block with a blank line," "give variables fully descriptive names," "keep functions tiny and single-purpose," and "program defensively" each push a measured axis toward the model: redundant comments, overblanking, 19-character spec-derived names, half-size template functions, and the triple null checks, empty catch blocks and try/except around operations that cannot fail that community linters flag as slop ([Readability Spectrum](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13280); [Cotroneo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21634); [deslop](https://github.com/dabit3/deslop)). The over-correction, inserting irregular whitespace or dead code to look human, is both gameable by design (Whodunit drops those features and loses 0.02 F1) and erased by any formatter, and insertion attacks are what plagiarism tools now normalize away ([Whodunit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04013); [Sağlam & Schmid 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20158)). The durable lever is context: calling the repository's helpers instead of built-ins, matching the file's existing decomposition granularity and comment density, commenting only what the code does not say, letting functions be as large as the surrounding code's, and leaving the mature-codebase traits (protected-member access, project-specific exception handling) that "encode context the generator did not have."

### What style can change and what it cannot

Everything in the two signal tables is stylometric or statistical and therefore within a writing style's reach, with one qualifier: per-token predictability is two-sided and scorer-relative, so the goal is the human band (wider vocabulary, idiosyncratic but coherent phrasing, content-driven rhythm) rather than maximal surprise, which registers as degenerate sampling. Originality in the plagiarism sense is equally controllable and has a precise definition: for prose, no run of eight or more consecutive normalized words that exists in an index unless it is quoted and cited, and no passage built by substituting synonyms into a source's function-word skeleton, which stop-word 8-grams and sentence tf-idf cosine ≥ 0.33 still catch; for code, no run of M (9–12) structural tokens in the same order as another program after identifiers, literals, comments and layout are stripped, which means renaming and reformatting someone else's code changes nothing in any tool while designing one's own control flow and decomposition produces low overlap even on standard problems. Memorized fragments are avoidable but not styleable: the exposure is sentence-length verbatim reproduction of heavily duplicated text at roughly 0.5–0.7% of unprompted generations in GPT-2 and rising with model size, so a guide should forbid completing famous openings, prefer composing definitions and boilerplate afresh or quoting them, and treat a clean similarity report as silent on close paraphrase of a specific source, which matchers miss at 0.42–0.72 recall.

Four channels are outside style's reach entirely. The keyed watermark on Claude output is default-on worldwide, covers Claude Code and the API, survives copy-paste and light editing, is lower-density in code and factual text, and is removed only by substantive regeneration, heavy paraphrase, translation or mixing, none of which a style rule performs; its detection is key-gated, so no third-party tool can read it, and its presence says nothing about how the text reads ([Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/16266773-how-claude-marks-ai-generated-content)). Verbatim-copy fingerprints of text that already exists are a property of the index, not the prose: Turnitin keeps historical copies of crawled pages and 1.9 billion student papers indefinitely, so deleting or editing a source does not remove it as a match. Provider-side retrieval would catch paraphrases at 97% if deployed, and metadata provenance is how AI code is actually attributed in 2026: Co-Authored-By trailers, "Generated with Claude Code" lines, bot identities and CLAUDE.md or .cursorrules files, at 75–92% precision, alongside Grammarly's Authorship logs and GPTZero's copy-paste and editing-time tracking ([Repository census 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24429); [Grammarly](https://www.grammarly.com/blog/company/ai-detector-authorship/)). The honest framing for a skill is therefore that it governs how prose and code read and whether they are composed rather than reproduced, while provenance, by design, remains visible to those holding the key or the repository.

## Conclusion

The literature's most useful reframing is that detectors of every kind are variance detectors. Zero-shot statistics look for a token-probability sequence that is too compact, supervised classifiers for a register that is too consistent, code detectors for layout and commentary that are too regular, and the 2025–26 alignment-imprint methods make the underlying cause explicit: instruction tuning "reallocates probability mass toward a narrower set of preferred responses," so an aligned model prefers its own text more than its base model does. The practical corollary inverts the folk goal. The target is not to be unpredictable but to be *wide*: to occupy more of the human distribution's degrees of freedom in vocabulary (including repetition where cohesion wants it), in grammar (passives, modals, plain copulas, concessive clauses), in structure (uneven paragraphs, abrupt endings, asides) and above all in content, because specificity, committed opinion, real quotations, unusual examples and moral ambiguity are the signals models decline by default and that no detector can learn to penalise without also penalising humans. Homogenization is the second, independent reason to pursue width: even undetectable AI prose measurably collapses the variance of a corpus and erases demographic signal, which matters for a writer who wants to sound like one particular person.

Three forward-looking implications follow. First, any tell list must carry a "last verified against model X on date Y" stamp, because the named markers turn over every generation and the 2026 artifacts (no dashes, 8–20-word sentences, plainer verbs) are themselves products of suppressing the 2024 ones. Second, provenance is replacing classification: with three providers watermarking, EU interoperability due in February 2027, and institutions from Vanderbilt to Curtin disabling classifier-based AI indicators, the question "does this read as human?" is separating from "was this generated?", and a style guide should answer only the first. Third, the only durable human signals are the expensive ones; everything cheap to imitate (contractions, warmth, typos, casual register) has already been imitated, catalogued and trained against, so a guide that spends its rules on content commitments rather than surface styling is the one that will still be right after the next model release.
