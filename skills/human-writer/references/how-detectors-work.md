# How detectors work: mechanisms, numbers, failure modes

Distilled from the full research report (`research-report.md`, October 2026), which carries the citations. Read this when you want to know *why* a rule in SKILL.md exists, or when someone asks how a particular tool decides.

Contents: 1 the three measurements; 2 zero-shot statistics; 3 trained classifiers; 4 where text detectors break; 5 code detectors; 6 plagiarism fingerprinting; 7 watermarks and provenance; 8 what style can and cannot change.

## 1. The three measurements

Every detector that matters reduces to one of three measurements:
1. **Surprise and its uniformity under a language model.** How probable each token is to a scorer, and how evenly that probability is spread. Zero-shot detectors threshold this directly; trained classifiers learn it together with the register that instruction tuning imprints.
2. **Survival of k-gram runs in an index.** Plagiarism checkers never model style. They normalize, hash every run of k tokens, keep a position-independent subset, and flag any run that already exists in their index.
3. **Correlation with a secret key.** Watermarks bias token choice in a keyed pseudorandom pattern that only the key holder can test.

A writing style can move the first and, by composing rather than reproducing, the second. It cannot touch the third.

## 2. Zero-shot statistics (no training; one or two scoring models)

All of them compute a function of per-token log-probability, rank or entropy under a scorer, then threshold.

- **GLTR (2019)**: colour each token by its rank under GPT-2 (top 10 green, top 100 yellow, top 1,000 red). Human text uses words outside the top 100 about 2.4x as often as GPT-2 text; the rank histogram alone gives AUC 0.87.
- **Perplexity and burstiness (GPTZero, January 2023)**: perplexity is exp(mean negative log-likelihood); burstiness is the variation of perplexity across sentences. The only published guideline: "a perplexity above 85 is more likely than not from a human source." GPTZero dropped both in autumn 2023 for a trained classifier. The causal demonstration of what they measured: seven detectors flagged 61% of non-native TOEFL essays versus 5% of US eighth-grade essays; the flagged essays had lower GPT-2 perplexity; asking ChatGPT to "enhance word choice" cut the false-positive rate to 12%, and simplifying the native essays raised theirs to 57%. Low lexical variety reads as machine.
- **DetectGPT (2023)**: d(x) = log p(x) minus the mean log p of about 100 T5 mask-and-refill rewrites. Machine text sits at a local maximum of probability, so rewrites lose likelihood; human text does not. AUROC about 0.97 on 2023 models, 0.62 on GPT-4, below chance on a 2024 benchmark. Costs ~100 generations per passage.
- **Fast-DetectGPT (2024)**: a z-score of the actual token's log-probability against the scorer's own sampled alternatives at each position, one forward pass, 340x faster, AUROC 0.96 on ChatGPT and 0.91 on GPT-4. Machine text is text whose every token is at least as likely as what the scorer would have drawn itself.
- **LRR and NPR (2023)**: ratio of log-likelihood to log-rank; ratio of log-rank after small perturbations to before. Rank is more discriminating than likelihood because it ignores how peaked the distribution is.
- **Binoculars (2024)**: B = log PPL(observer) / log cross-PPL(observer, performer), with Falcon-7B and Falcon-7B-instruct. The denominator estimates how surprising the text should be given an unknown prompt (the "capybara problem": an odd prompt makes machine text look surprising). Thresholds in the reference code: 0.9015 (best F1) or 0.8536 (0.01% FPR); scores below threshold are AI; input truncated to 512 tokens.
- **Alignment-imprint methods (2025 to 2026)**: compare an instruction-tuned model's likelihood with its base model's (LAPD, IRM). Alignment "reallocates probability mass toward a narrower set of preferred responses"; an aligned model prefers its own text more than its base does. Average AUROC 92 versus 82 for Fast-DetectGPT and 84 for Binoculars.
- **Structure-based**: DNA-GPT regenerates from a truncated prefix and counts n-gram overlap; Luminol shuffles sentences and watches perplexity rise 4 to 5x for machine text versus 20 to 60% for human (local coherence without global planning); Lastde measures how abruptly the surprisal sequence fluctuates; intrinsic dimension of token embeddings is about 9.5 for human English and 8 for AI text regardless of generator.

Two properties of this family matter for writing. The signal is two-sided: random tokens and overheated sampling also read as "human", and Binoculars collapses to AUROC 0.02 to 0.07 when a generator samples above temperature 1 or applies a repetition penalty. And the scorer defines the verdict: the same statistic scored 0.93 or 0.51 on Kazakh text depending on which small scorer was used.

## 3. Trained classifiers (what the commercial tools run)

- **OpenAI's GPT-2 detector (RoBERTa, 2019)**: 95% on 510-token GPT-2 samples; by 2025 it labelled 90 to 98% of human passages as AI. **OpenAI's 2023 classifier**: 26% true positives, 9% false positives, withdrawn after six months.
- **Ghostbuster (2023)**: runs the document through a unigram model, a trigram model and two GPT-3 models, searches over compositions of the aligned per-token probability vectors, and feeds the result to logistic regression. Removing the GPT-3 features drops F1 from 99 to 88; seven handcrafted features alone give 80.5. The decisive signal is the relation between how predictable each token is under models of different strength, not any lexical tell. Unreliable below 100 tokens; plateaus near 500.
- **Turnitin**: splits a submission into overlapping segments of roughly five to ten sentences, scores each sentence 0 to 1, pools, and averages into a document percentage. Minimum 300 words of English prose; lists and bullets excluded; scores under 20% carry an asterisk because of false positives; the document-level "under 1% FPR" claim applies only above 20%, and its own sentence-level FPR is 4%. Its VP of AI described the target as writing that is "too consistently average" and noted that formulaic human registers (economics, lab reports) get flagged for the same reason. A 2025 model was trained on the output of commercial "humanizer" tools.
- **GPTZero (current)**: an end-to-end deep classifier with human / mixed / AI probabilities, a hidden-Markov sentence highlighter, subclasses for AI-polished and AI-paraphrased text, and an output mapping that "favors false negatives over false positives." Architecture proprietary.
- **Pangram (the only vendor with real disclosure)**: a transformer trained on about 28 million pre-2021 human documents, each paired with a "synthetic mirror" matched in topic, length and style, with hard-negative mining that cut Wikipedia false positives from 5.3% to 0.23%. Pangram 4 (July 2026) emits token-wise human / AI-assisted / AI-generated labels decoded with a CRF, and keeps a separate humanizer-detection head because training it into the backbone "concentrated errors in academic writing and ESL essays." Vendor figures: FPR 0.0041% on over a million human texts, FNR 0.34%, 98% of humanized text caught, AI-polished human text flagged 0.01% of the time by design.
- **Originality.ai**: an ELECTRA-style discriminator fine-tuned on millions of samples at varied temperatures; reports an estimated share of AI text.

Because mirrors are topic-matched, these models cannot learn topic. What remains is probability structure and register: evaluative vocabulary stacked on every noun, nominalizations, participial tails, contrastive framing, uniform paragraphs, tidy previews and wrap-ups, generic names, no quotes or citations. That is also the profile of formulaic human prose, which is where the false positives live.

## 4. Where text detectors break, measured

- **Decoding**: a repetition penalty of 1.2 cut every RAID detector by up to 32 points; across 37 decoding settings Binoculars went from 0.99 at temperature 0.5 to 0.002 at 1.3.
- **Paraphrase**: a paraphraser (DIPPER) cut DetectGPT from 70% to 5% detection at 1% FPR, yet on RAID the same paraphraser left Binoculars unchanged and *raised* Originality from 85 to 97, because paraphrasers have their own fingerprints. Synonym swapping dropped Binoculars 36 points; homoglyphs collapsed Originality to 9% with only GPTZero immune.
- **Generator drift**: Fast-DetectGPT fell from 0.91 on Claude-3-haiku to 0.65 on Claude-3.5-haiku; newer models within a family are harder; base models are harder than chat models.
- **Predictable humans**: Binoculars scored 38 AUROC, below chance, on Australian legal text; the US Constitution scores on the machine side; the GPT-2 detector flagged up to 8.7% of real pre-ChatGPT abstracts; posts from autism-focused subreddits were flagged 25% more often without having lower perplexity. A 2026 replication found no non-native bias in current detector families and no perplexity gap, and Pangram reports 0% FPR on TOEFL essays, so the bias is a property of surprisal-based and older classifiers.
- **Length**: three independent curves put stable verdicts at 250 to 500 words. Ghostbuster plateaus near 500 tokens, Turnitin needs 300 words, Pangram excludes anything under 50.
- **Mixing**: expert human editing of 20 to 40% of a machine text cut Binoculars from 84 to 61 AUROC; LLM polishing of the same text was still caught at 78 to 80. Replacing a quarter of a machine text with human text lowered zero-shot AUROC by 20%; inserting machine text into human text by only 4%.
- **Independent audits**: 2023, best of 14 tools (Turnitin) 76% binary accuracy, machine-paraphrased text caught 15% of the time; 2024, seven detectors averaged 40% on unmodified GPT-4/Claude 2/Bard text; 2025 (Chicago Booth), Pangram FPR 0 to 0.1% with FNR 0.5 to 4%, GPTZero FPR 0.7%, Originality FNR 10 to 42%, and a commercial humanizer pushed GPTZero's miss rate to 26 to 77% while Pangram stayed at or below 5%. Vendor-versus-vendor numbers contradict each other.
- **Theory**: a single-sample detector's AUROC is bounded by the total-variation distance between machine and human text, but the distance grows with sample length, so any positive gap is detectable given enough text. Short text is the universal blind spot.
- **Policy**: Vanderbilt disabled Turnitin's AI indicator in August 2023 (750 of 75,000 papers would be false positives at the claimed rate); trackers list dozens of institutions by 2026; OpenAI calls detectors "not reliable enough."

## 5. Code detectors

Code's grammar makes most tokens forced: over 70% of code tokens have entropy below 1, so GLTR and DetectGPT lose about 32 AUROC points moving from text to code, and perplexity works for C/C++ (~91% AUC) but not Python (~64%). General text detectors are uncalibrated on code: GPTZero called essentially all student Python AI (true-negative rate 0.0016); DetectGPT called most of it human.

Methods that work perturb or condition on the stylistic residue: DetectCodeGPT inserts random spaces and newlines and measures the change in log-rank (whitespace is the token class with the biggest machine-human gap; AUROC 0.83); ATC conditions entropy on an inferred task description and excludes comments (AUROC ~93); code-rewriting detectors measure how little an LLM changes synthetic code. Trained detectors learn surface regularity: average line length is the top feature in the best-known stylometric model (higher means GPT-4), seven whitespace features alone reach 88%, and encoders reach 0.95 to 0.99 in-distribution. All collapse under shift: 82 to 43 F1 across settings, 33% recall on human-edited hybrid code, TPR at 1% FPR under 0.3 on a 10-language benchmark, 98 to 25 macro-F1 on SemEval-2026's cross-language test. In practice AI code is attributed in 2026 by git metadata (bot accounts, Co-Authored-By trailers, CLAUDE.md files), not by content.

## 6. Plagiarism fingerprinting

The skeleton (winnowing, 2003): normalize (lowercase, strip whitespace and punctuation, map identifiers to one symbol for code), cut into k-grams, hash each with a Karp-Rabin rolling hash, slide a window of w hashes and keep the minimum (rightmost on ties), index fingerprint to (document, offset). Guarantee: any shared substring of length w + k - 1 is detected; nothing shorter than k can be; about 2/(w + 1) of hashes are kept. A second pass (suffix arrays, greedy string tiling) finds exact passage boundaries and merges adjacent matches. Resemblance is Jaccard over shingle sets; containment is shared shingles over one document's shingles, which is what plagiarism needs. Web-scale near-duplicate detection uses MinHash with LSH banding or 64-bit SimHash at Hamming distance 3.

Tool floors: Turnitin 8 consecutive words (default and minimum; 70 billion crawled pages with historical copies, 1.9 billion student papers, publisher content); iThenticate 9; Unicheck 4; PlagScan about 3; PlagAware 50 characters; JPlag 9 to 12 structural tokens depending on language; Dolos any shared run of 39 syntax-tree tokens; Compare50 k = 25, t = 35 with identifiers mapped to "v". Scores are the share of words (or tokens) in matched runs. Turnitin says the score "is not an assessment of whether the paper includes plagiarized material"; institutions that set thresholds use 15 to 20% overall with 1 to 2% per source.

Paraphrase (PAN competitions): seeds are built to survive specific edits (sorted n-grams and skip-grams for reordering, stop-word 8-grams for synonym swaps, sentence cosine above 0.33 for heavy rewording); extension merges seeds across gaps; filtering drops short passages. Best system: plagdet 0.90 verbatim, 0.88 synonym-and-shuffle, 0.89 machine back-translation, 0.56 human summary. Commercial tools in the last independent test (15 tools, 2020): synonym replacement partially caught, manual paraphrase caught by none satisfactorily, translation caught by one. Homoglyphs and hidden text are mapped back and flagged. Intrinsic (style-shift) detection has precision about 0.2 and is triage only.

Memorization: GPT-2 emitted verbatim training spans of 256+ characters in 0.5 to 0.7% of unprompted generations; GPT-J reproduces at least 1% of its training set given 50-token prompts; the rate grows with model size, duplication and prompt length. The exposure for a writer is sentence-length fragments of heavily duplicated text, not the document percentage.

Code details (MOSS, JPlag, Dolos, obfuscation, convergence) are in `originality.md`.

## 7. Watermarks and provenance

- **Green-list (Kirchenbauer 2023)**: hash the previous token to split the vocabulary into a green list (fraction γ) and red list; add δ to green logits; detect with z = (greens - γT) / sqrt(Tγ(1-γ)), rejecting at z > 4. Raises perplexity from 5.1 to 6.2 at δ = 2. Weakened but not erased by paraphrase; humans asked to remove it by hand were still detected after about 800 tokens.
- **SynthID-Text (Google, Nature 2024)**: g-values from a hash of the previous four tokens plus the key; 30 layers of pairwise tournaments among candidates; non-distortionary configuration left Gemini feedback rates unchanged across 20 million responses; weaker when entropy is low (lower temperature, larger models, factual text, code).
- **textGrain (OpenAI, October 2026)**: keyed vocabulary blocks coupled through entropy-budgeted optimal transport; detector sum is Gamma-distributed under the null; 10% synonym replacement cuts detection from 92% to 66%.
- **Deployment, October 2026**: Google in the Gemini app since 2024; Anthropic default-on worldwide for all Claude models launched from 2 August 2026, covering Claude, the API, Claude Code, Cowork and Tag, with detection in private preview for regulators, media, researchers and educators; OpenAI rolling out in the EU with worldwide API opt-in. No confirmed watermark for Copilot, Llama, Mistral or Grok. Every deployed detector is key-gated: GPTZero and Turnitin cannot read any of them. EU cross-provider interoperability is due 2 February 2027.
- **What removes one**: regeneration by another model, heavy paraphrase, translation, mixing with other text. What does not: style edits, light editing, copy-paste. Anthropic phrases a positive as "may have been generated or processed by Claude" because editing human text with the model also marks it; proofreading "mostly preserves human words, so the watermark may be too sparse to detect."
- **Code**: hardest medium; selective schemes watermark only high-entropy tokens and lose most of the signal under renaming, dead-code insertion and try/catch wrapping.
- **Other provenance**: provider-side retrieval (store every generation, match later) would catch 97% of paraphrases but is not deployed; C2PA covers images, audio, video, PDF and office formats, not plain text or source code; GPTZero logs copy-paste and editing time and Grammarly Authorship records writing provenance; AI code is attributed by git metadata.

A watermark positive and a stylometric verdict are independent channels with uncorrelated errors. "Humanizing" style leaves a watermark intact; regenerating with a non-watermarking model removes it regardless of how machine-like the result reads. A watermark negative says nothing about authorship.

## 8. What style can and cannot change

Within reach: every stylometric and statistical signal (predictability and its flatness, register, evaluative density, structural uniformity, specificity, punctuation habits) and originality in the fingerprint sense (no uncited 8-word runs from an index; no synonym-substituted copies of a source's skeleton; for code, no runs of a dozen structural tokens in another program's order).

Out of reach: the keyed watermark on Claude output; verbatim fingerprints of text that already exists in an index (Turnitin keeps historical copies, so editing a source online does not remove it); provider-side retrieval; git and platform metadata.

The reframing the literature supports: detectors of every kind are variance detectors. The target is not to be unpredictable but to be wide: to occupy more of the human distribution in vocabulary (including repetition where cohesion wants it), grammar (passives, modals, plain copulas, concessive clauses), structure (uneven paragraphs, abrupt endings, asides) and above all content (specifics, committed opinion, real quotations, unusual examples, moral ambiguity), which are the signals models decline by default and that no detector can penalize without penalizing humans. Everything cheap to imitate (contractions, warmth, typos, casual register) has already been imitated, catalogued and trained against.
